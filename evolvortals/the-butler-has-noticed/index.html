The Butler Has Noticed

The door learns how you play, but you decide what it remembers.

The mantelpiece

One curiosity for every recorded burglary. Burning a page of Pell’s file does not take anything off this shelf.

    How to play

    Each burglary is a short, turn-based heist with no clock. Choose a way in, find your way to the Display Room, open the vitrine, take tonight’s curiosity and get back out to the lawn.

    The vitrine can be opened three ways: pick its lock, fetch its key, or ring a bell elsewhere so the night footman leaves it open. Scouting a room is optional.

    Mr. Ambrose Pell, the butler, watches. He notices exactly four things:

    • which side you came in by,
    • how you opened the vitrine,
    • whether you scouted a room before taking the prize,
    • whether you left the way you came in.

    After one burglary he rebuilds the Hall against what you just did. After that he acts on any choice you made in two of your last three burglaries. His rule is printed on the first page of his dossier, so you can use it against him: repeat a choice to draw him one way, then do something else.

    In Pell’s Study you can steal one page of his file per burglary. Burn it and it is gone for good. Mark it “Disregard” and it stays readable but stops counting; you can let him regard it again between burglaries.

    After three recorded burglaries the Great Burglary opens. You may do up to five ordinary burglaries first; after five, the Great Burglary is next. Its Hall is built from every belief left in the file.

    “Pell’s evening off” is a burglary nobody records, in the original Hall. Taking a long break changes nothing: Pell’s file waits exactly as you left it.

    Every choice is a button. The text map lists every room, passage and change, so you can play without the picture: turn on “Text first” in Settings.

    Settings and saving

    What is stored, and where

    Only the game itself: the burglaries you finished (as the moves you made, so the file can be checked when it loads), the changes you made to Pell’s file, and these three settings. It stays in this browser on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere, there are no accounts, and no times, dates or device details are recorded.

    Burn the whole file?

    This erases every page, belief, change, curiosity on the mantelpiece and the Great Burglary, and returns Thornwick Hall to the first night. Your settings stay. It cannot be undone.
