Twelve Clocks Till Midnight

Through the workshop's tall windows, the plaza fountain lies dark.

The clockmaker's note

It is 6:00 PM. The old clockmaker has gone home, leaving the workshop to you. Twelve clocks line the wall, and none of them agree. Pinned to the bench is a card in a careful hand:

“They needn't merely agree once. They must agree when midnight comes.”

One instrument here is trustworthy: the Reference Time. Move it forward and back, watch how each clock strays, write down what you see, and correct each clock's hands (what it shows) and regulator (how fast it runs). Then prove your work with a Test Run that checks every clock across the whole evening.

Four clocks wait on the bench first. Nothing here needs sound, speed or real waiting: time only moves when you move it.

Test Run

A Test Run checks every active clock at five moments: 6:00 PM, 8:30 PM, 10:15 PM, 12:00 AM (midnight) and 12:30 AM. A clock passes only if it matches the reference to the second at all five, so a clock that is right once (or right only at midnight) will not pass.

Success Ledger

Help & workshop rules

How clocks go wrong (offset vs. rate)

Offset: the clock runs at the right speed but its hands were set wrong. Its difference from the reference is the same at every moment. Fix it by moving the hands.

Rate: the clock runs too fast or too slow. Its difference grows by the same amount every hour. “Fast by 2 min per hour” means that after 3 hours it has gained 6 minutes. Fix it with the regulator: a clock that gains 2 min per hour needs the regulator set to −2.

A clock can have both. Then it may agree with the reference at exactly one moment and be wrong at every other. That's why one matching reading proves nothing.

How corrections work

All corrections are made at the bench as of the 6:00 PM opening. Hands shift the clock's reading at 6:00 PM by whole minutes (up to ±12 hours). The regulator changes how many minutes per hour the clock gains (+) or loses (−) from 6:00 PM onward, in whole minutes per hour (−10 to +10).

Because corrections apply from 6:00 PM, the readings you see at every reference time update immediately when you adjust. Move the reference back and forth to check: the same reference time always gives the same reading.

A few advanced clocks have one extra fitting, such as a mainspring, a dial or a window. Its plate or note tells you about it.

Reading the clocks

The text reading under each dial is authoritative. Every clock in this workshop has a small day/night window, so readings include AM or PM. A clock exactly 12 hours wrong looks perfect on its dial but shows the wrong AM/PM, and its difference says so. Seconds matter: a small drift may show up only as seconds at first.

Proof, changes and saving

A stage counts as verified only while the latest Test Run passed it and no hands, regulator or fitting has changed since. If you change a setting afterwards, your earned result stays in the Success Ledger as past proof, but the workshop shows it as not verified until you run the test again. Moving the reference time, pinning notes, reading hints and bench checks never change verification.
