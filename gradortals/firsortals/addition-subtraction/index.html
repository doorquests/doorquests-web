          What the child is learning

          Making ten. Ten is a useful group. The ferry has exactly ten seats in two rows of five, so a full ferry is always 10. When 8 friends are aboard and 5 more arrive, 2 fill the ferry (8 + 2 = 10) and the other 3 ride the raft. So 8 + 5 = 10 + 3 = 13. Nobody is added or lost; friends only change places.

          Two ways to show 13 − 5 = 8

          • Raft first: the 3 raft friends step off, then 2 ferry friends. 13 − 3 = 10, then 10 − 2 = 8.
          • Ferry first: 5 ferry friends step off. 10 − 5 = 5 stay in seats, and 3 are still on the raft. 5 + 3 = 8.

          Any other mix of 5 also leaves 8, and the page shows why. No way is marked best.

          Helpful things to say (without solving it)

          • “How many seats are empty? How do you know?”
          • “The ferry is full. Where can the others ride?”
          • “How many are still aboard? Can you count them another way?”
          • “Could you have done it a different way?”

          What this page does not do

          This is a learning activity, not a test. The journey log records taps and moves only. It does not measure understanding, and it keeps no scores, names, or timings. Read aloud uses your device’s own voice when one is available; its sound quality has not been checked. Captain Moss shows the first six riders himself, so that part is a model, not the child’s own work.
