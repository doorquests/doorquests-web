        For grown-ups: what this teaches and how the pretend garden works

        The big idea

        Plants need suitable conditions: water, air around their roots, warmth, and, once a seedling is up, light. The child sets light and water for three sunflower seeds, rings a bell to move forward one simulated day at a time, notices differences, and changes conditions to help.

        Science the garden shows

        • Seeds can sprout in the dark. A sunflower seed does not need light to germinate. It needs moisture, air (oxygen) and warmth, and it uses food stored inside the seed. A seed on the dim shelf can sprout.
        • Seedlings need light to keep growing well. Once the stored food is used, a seedling makes food using light. In dim light it grows pale, thin and stretched, leaning toward light. In the bright window it turns green and grows on.
        • Roots need air too. Soggy, waterlogged soil fills the air spaces, so roots get too little oxygen and the plant can droop. Soggy soil drains over a day or two after watering is reduced, and the plant can perk up.
        • Too little water leaves the soil dry: seeds wait, and plants droop until watered.
        • Fair comparing. "Compare the pots" says when only one condition differed (a good clue) and when light and water both differed (we cannot tell which one mattered).
        • Life cycle. After a clearly marked jump to later in the season, the sunflowers bloom, bees and other insects visit the flowers (pollination), seeds ripen, finches eat some seeds, and Odo keeps one to plant again. Finches eat seeds; they are not the pollinators.

        How the model is simplified (please read)

        • This is a storybook simulation, not gardening advice and not a calibrated plant model. Stages, timing and responses are simplified and pending review by an early-science educator.
        • Each bell ring is one simulated day, not real time. Real sunflowers often take about a week or more to come up and a couple of months or more to flower. The jump to "later in the growing season" stands for many more days.
        • Light and water are cartoon levels (window or shelf; a sip, just enough, too much), not measurements. Warmth is always suitable inside the bus.
        • Deliberate simplifications: a seed in soggy soil simply waits (real seeds in waterlogged soil can also rot); the dim shelf is too dim for a seedling to keep growing (real seedlings in low light grow slowly and leggy); a thirsty or soggy plant takes a simulated day or two to recover (real wilting from dryness can recover within hours). No plant in this garden ever dies.
        • Real plants vary. The garden says "can" and "often" where that is true.

        Reading and listening

        No reading is required, but there is no recorded narration in this prototype. Read Odo's words aloud, or turn on "Device voice", which uses your browser's built-in speech. Device voices vary, may be silent on some devices, and some need a network connection. Everything important is also shown in pictures and text.

        Privacy

        Nothing is sent anywhere. There are no accounts, ads, analytics, cookies, microphone or camera. Progress for this garden is saved only on this device, under its own key, and "Start a new garden" clears only that key. Saved progress on a device can be edited by someone who knows how; this game re-checks it, but it is not tamper-proof.
