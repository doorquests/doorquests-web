Prototype status: a Founder-playtest prototype. Not finalized, not classroom-tested, not published.

What this story practices

An invitation is an offer, not a demand. Your child picks a picture invitation, knocks, waits, and listens. Each neighbor answers in their own way, and every answer is treated as a good, complete answer. At dusk, a neighbor knocks on your child's own door, and your child chooses the answer.

Yes
They play or visit together.
No
A whole answer. Nobody needs to explain it or change it.
Later
It means maybe, not a delayed yes. Asking again is a new question that can get a new answer.
Watch
Being nearby and watching is a real way to take part. Nobody is pulled in.
Alone time
One neighbor happily chooses to be alone. That isn't sad or unfriendly.

The neighbors

Bramble the hedgehog loves lively play. Mo the otter talks with picture cards and often has a different idea. Tansy the mouse says "later." Wick the owl likes to watch from the window. Rue the rabbit enjoys being alone with a book.

How to help

Read the words aloud, point to the pictures, and let your child make each choice, including letting a door be. Please don't steer them toward "yes" or toward visiting everyone. There are no points or scores, and nothing is counted. Every ending is complete, whether many neighbors gather or none do.

Sound and read aloud

Both are off by default. Sounds are short, soft tones made by the browser. Read aloud uses your device's built-in voice, so it varies by device. It is not recorded narration, and it may be missing on some devices. All words always appear on screen.

Privacy and saving
