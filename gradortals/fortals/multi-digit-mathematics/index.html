    Chapter II The Drawers That Cannot Lie

    Registrar Odalys Penn

    Every drawer holds one kind of piece. A thousand-bundle holds ten hundred-bundles. A hundred-bundle holds ten ten-bundles. A ten-bundle holds ten single sacks.

    The shipment slip asks for 8 single sacks — but look: the ones drawer has only 3. Let's see what the drawers allow.

    Your task

    Get at least 8 sacks into the ones drawer without losing a single sack. Try anything: the drawers will tell you when a move breaks the rule.

    1. Choose a drawer.
    2. Open Bundle unties one bundle so you can see the ten pieces inside. Nothing moves yet.
    3. Move Ten carries all ten pieces into the next drawer down, together.

    Chapter complete

    Chapter IV Check the Ledger

    Registrar Odalys Penn

    A careful registrar checks a subtraction a second way. If 2,745 sacks stayed and 1,258 sacks left, then putting the shipment back must rebuild exactly 4,003. Not 4,002. Not 4,103.

    Required: the inverse check

    Add what stayed and what left. You can work it out on paper first.

    Another way: count up on the number line Optional

    Start at the shipment, 1,258, and jump forward until you land on 4,003. Friendly numbers — the next ten, the next hundred, the next thousand — make good landing spots. The total of your jumps is the difference.

    Another way: compensation Optional

    Add the same amount to both numbers and the gap between them stays the same. This changes the problem, not the granary: the drawers keep exactly the same sacks.
