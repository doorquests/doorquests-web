## Premise

Margo Finch has worked at the Lost Property Office beneath Halsey Street Station for twelve years. Four objects arrive during a single working day: a wedding ring deliberately left on a train, an urn whose rightful return is disputed by two siblings, a briefcase belonging to a missing husband, and a sketchbook connected to Margo's own abandoned dreams. Each case asks whether returning an object is the same as helping its owner.

## Four cases

1. **Item 4471 — Gold wedding ring:** Richard Ashworth requests his wife Anna's ring. A note tucked into its napkin suggests she left intentionally; contacting Anna reveals her wishes.
2. **Item 4472 — Urn marked DAD:** Graham and Lorna Kerr both want their father's ashes. A message under the urn asks them to scatter the ashes at Loch Fyne together.
3. **Item 4473 — Leather briefcase:** Clare Ainsley is searching for her missing husband Martin. Martin calls the office and asks not to be found. His daughter Ellie is part of what makes the decision difficult.
4. **Item N-0912 — Blue sketchbook:** From a closed branch's auction box, Margo discovers her own sketchbook, left behind twelve years earlier on the day she abandoned a possible art-college future.

## Presentation and interface

The visual design recreates a railway lost-property counter. Four pigeonholes display the objects and their stamped disposition tags. A station clock progresses from **07:30**, while counters track the number of closed cases and bent rules. The **Book of Found Things** records each item's fate beside **The Rules**, supervisor Mr. Delahaye's laminated instructions.

The visual identity retains station green, manila tags, brass and rubber-stamp red, alongside Libre Franklin, Libre Baskerville and Special Elite typefaces. The responsive layout includes collapsible reference panels on narrow screens.

## Rules and decisions

Mr. Delahaye's five written rules require proof of ownership, limit opening sealed containers, send unclaimed objects to auction after 90 days, prohibit staff claiming objects for themselves, and prohibit revealing owner details to others.

- Each scene presents three numbered choices; back and restart controls allow exploration.
- The story tracks **four case dispositions**, a **care score** for compassionate resolutions in the first three cases, and a **bent-rule total**.
- Contacting Anna, arranging the siblings' joint collection, and relaying Martin's message without disclosing his location are the three compassionate case resolutions.
- Choosing what happens to Margo's sketchbook shapes the end of her own story.
- Three bent rules trigger Mr. Delahaye's disciplinary response.
- Browser-local progress restoration and persistent ending discovery support repeat play.

## Six endings

1. **Everything in Its Place** — the truest ending: the three compassionate case resolutions are reached, and Margo claims her own sketchbook without crossing the three-rule threshold.
2. **Most Things Returned** — the day concludes without resolving every person's underlying need.
3. **Mr. Delahaye Notices** — three or more rules have been bent.
4. **Unclaimed** — Margo lets her sketchbook go to auction.
5. **Passed On** — Margo gives her sketchbook to an aspiring artist.
6. **Gone Again** — Margo releases Martin's briefcase to Clare despite his request.

The ending selector gives priority to **Mr. Delahaye Notices** when the bent-rule threshold is reached, followed by **Passed On** or **Unclaimed** when those sketchbook choices apply. Otherwise, the care score distinguishes the two remaining closing-time outcomes. **Gone Again** branches directly from the briefcase case.
