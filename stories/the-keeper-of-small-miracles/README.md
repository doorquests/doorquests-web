> Each day you may grant seven tiny miracles, knowing even small interventions can change lives.

## Premise

On a hospice bed on **Saint Bride's Row**, a very old woman takes your hand. Something impossibly small and warm passes into your palm: **seven sparks**.

**Mrs. Abernathy**, ninety-four, kept the Row's miracles for **fifty-one years**. You have been the new Keeper for fourteen hours when a grey Tuesday begins. A seventeen-year-old's alarm has failed to ring; an exhausted nurse is about to drive home; and a little streetful of people carry private worries that might connect in unexpected ways.

The player can cause only **tiny, unprovable miracles**. A pigeon tapping a window, a bus forty seconds late, the right song appearing on the radio or a lucky gust of wind may change a life. The story asks whether the greatest miracle is helping each person separately—or bringing them together.

## Mrs. Abernathy's five rules

1. **Seven a day.** Sparks do not carry over past midnight.
2. **Nothing bigger than a sneeze.** A pigeon, a gust, a traffic signal, a song.
3. **Nothing anybody could prove.**
4. **No raising the dead, no lottery numbers, and no making anyone love anyone.**
5. **Watch where it goes afterward.** A small intervention always has consequences.

An optional opening conversation with Mrs. Abernathy adds the central advice: **pick the miracles that touch two lives, and save a spark for the night**.

## How to play

Every choice appears in a group of three; click or tap an option or press **1**, **2**, or **3**. Each miracle displays its **spark cost** and occasionally a hint about possible effects. Choosing **Let it be** costs nothing.

**Retrace your last step** undoes a choice, while **Start over** begins Tuesday again. The browser remembers previously discovered endings; the reviewed website HTML also saves the current scene and undo history.

A choice costing more sparks than remain is visibly unavailable and does not spend resources. An ordinary miracle generally costs **one spark**; exceptional interventions may cost **two**. The seven available sparks reset only when beginning a new day or replaying.

## Signature mechanics

### Seven Sparks

Seven illustrated flames under the title show the remaining miracle budget. Each expenditure extinguishes the corresponding flame; all unused sparks expire at midnight. The flames have a subtle flicker that is disabled under reduced-motion preferences.

The first optional practice choice—making a dandelion clock form a perfect circle—costs **one spark without creating a connection**. It demonstrates the mechanic but makes the strongest route impossible for that run.

### Ripples

Conditional narrative passages titled **“A ripple from earlier”** show how earlier choices affect later scenes. For instance, a forty-second bus delay places a retired professor beside a nervous student, and a song on the radio turns out to belong to a musician met only later.

These passages are evidence of the story's linked consequences, not separate endings.

### The Web

A circular diagram represents **seven residents of Saint Bride's Row**, arranged around St Bride's itself. When a choice creates a relationship, a **golden thread** connects two people. Characters appear faint until encountered, and relationships can reach people the reader has not met yet.

When the bells ring, **rose spokes** appear from the church toward the community. A separate status note displays the number of established connections and whether the entire seven-person graph is connected.

**Important:** Merely giving everyone an individual miracle does not connect the Web. The strongest ending requires a **single connected network**, not seven unrelated acts of kindness.

### The Keeper's Notebook

A running record lists each choice by encounter time, the miracle granted or intentionally withheld, and the spark cost. **Let it be** moments remain visible alongside the miracles, emphasizing that restraint is also a decision.

## Tuesday's seven encounters

### 06:40 — Jonah, seventeen

Jonah's phone has died and he may miss the **No. 9 bus** to his university interview. A pigeon can wake him, or a **forty-second delay at the depot** can let both Jonah and an elderly woman—**Margit**—catch it. Margit is a retired professor, and their conversation helps Jonah prepare for his interview.

### 07:30 — Pilar, forty-four

After fourteen hours as a hospice nurse, Pilar is struggling to remain awake while driving home. A run of green traffic lights could help, but playing **her favorite song on the radio** connects her to **Theo**, a local busker who recorded the song.

### 09:30 — Mr. Okafor, seventy-one

Widowed Mr. Okafor wants to bake a cake in memory of his wife **Grace** on what would have been her seventieth birthday. Her lost recipe could reappear, or **Vera**, the bakery-café owner, could remember that Grace used to order the cake there for her sister. The latter choice creates a relationship between Okafor and Vera.

### 12:15 — Saoirse, eight

Saoirse's dog **Biscuit** has wandered away and is asleep near the bakery. A bark would guide her to him directly; a **gust carrying her missing-dog poster** to Mr. Okafor creates a connection between the child and the widower. His care for Biscuit becomes an invitation into her family's life.

### 15:00 — Vera and Margit

Vera and Margit are sisters, estranged for **eleven years** over their mother's house. If the earlier bus delay helped Margit, she appears at Vera's café by chance. Either **Vera remembering Margit's coffee-and-toast order** or **their mother's song on the café radio** can reunite them for one spark.

Without the bus connection, bringing them together requires a **two-spark wrong-number intervention**—a costly alternative that does not fit the optimal seven-spark plan.

### 18:30 — Theo, twenty-nine

Theo is busking for the final time before a call-center job in Leeds. Filling his guitar case with coins helps him briefly. Arranging for **Jonah to stop and really listen** creates a bond between them—and reveals that a song from Theo's set played on the radio earlier that day.

### 22:00 — Mrs. Abernathy, ninety-four

The dying former Keeper wishes she could hear the bells of **St Bride's**, silent since 1990. The final choice is to ring them **once** for one spark, give her **one pain-free hour** for two sparks, stay and hold her hand, or attempt the forbidden miracle of making her well.

The strongest ending rings the bells **after** connecting the entire street.

## The Web of six connecting threads — spoilers

| One-spark intervention | Connection formed | Ripple |
|---|---|---|
| Delay the No. 9 bus forty seconds | **Jonah ↔ Margit** | Margit helps Jonah prepare for his interview |
| Play Pilar's favorite song | **Pilar ↔ Theo** | Theo's recording reaches someone who needs it |
| Have Vera remember Grace's cake | **Mr. Okafor ↔ Vera** | They prepare Grace's cake in Vera's kitchen |
| Blow Saoirse's poster toward the bakery | **Saoirse ↔ Mr. Okafor** | Biscuit is found and the two become friends |
| Vera remembers Margit's usual order **or** their mother's song plays | **Margit ↔ Vera** | The sisters begin to reconcile |
| Jonah stops to hear Theo | **Jonah ↔ Theo** | The musician gains a sincere listener |

All seven people are now connected by **six relationships**, the minimum number of edges needed to connect seven separate individuals. The final bell costs the **seventh spark**.

The source Markdown describes **“only one combination”**, but the canonical HTML provides **two valid fifth-encounter choices** (the remembered order or the mother's song), each creating the same Margit–Vera link for one spark. The required **network and resource pattern** is unique, while that scene has two interchangeable options.

## Six canonical endings

| ID | Ending | Narrative outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **The Bells of St Bride's** · *truest* | Ring the bells with the **entire seven-person Web connected**. Everyone experiences one shared, transformative moment. |
| **E2** | **Seven Kindnesses** | Ring the bells without connecting everyone; the street hears them, but some people remain alone. |
| **E3** | **The Keeper's Hour** | Spend two sparks to give Mrs. Abernathy an hour without pain. A loving but different gift from the bells. |
| **E4** | **Let It Be** | Hold her hand while unused sparks expire at midnight. |
| **E5** | **Empty Hands** | Arrive with no sparks remaining and regret being unable to ring the bells. |
| **E6** | **Too Big** | Attempt to make Mrs. Abernathy well, exceeding the power's fundamental restrictions; the Keeper's gift transfers elsewhere. |

### Exact strongest-ending rule

The canonical HTML resolves the bell choice by evaluating whether the **seven-person graph is connected**, using a graph traversal from Jonah that visits connected neighbors. It returns **E1** only when all seven people are reachable, and **E2** otherwise.

The bell option is available only when **at least one spark remains**. In normal play, connecting all seven people requires the six one-spark thread choices above; therefore this route arrives with **exactly one spark** to spend on the bells.

The story's alternative endings depend on the specific final action and available spark balance. The two-spark hour of relief requires at least two remaining sparks, while staying beside Mrs. Abernathy is free.

## Walkthrough to the truest ending — spoilers

1. At the opening, **ask Mrs. Abernathy one last question** (optional but narratively useful). Do **not** spend a spark on the dandelion demonstration.
2. **06:40:** Delay the **No. 9 bus** by forty seconds. This connects Jonah and Margit.
3. **07:30:** Play **Pilar's favorite song** on the radio, creating the Pilar–Theo thread.
4. **09:30:** Let **Vera remember Grace's cake**, linking Vera and Mr. Okafor.
5. **12:15:** Blow **Saoirse's lost-dog poster** toward the bakery, linking her with Mr. Okafor.
6. **15:00:** Have Vera remember **Margit's order** (or play their mother's song). Either option connects the sisters.
7. **18:30:** Let **Jonah stop to listen to Theo**, completing the connected Web.
8. **22:00:** Spend the **final spark** ringing the bells of St Bride's.

At the final encounter the Keeper has spent six sparks and created six threads. Ringing the bells expends the seventh and awards **E1 — The Bells of St Bride's**.

In the ending, Mrs. Abernathy dies peacefully after hearing the bells; the seven sparks return to the new Keeper's hand the next morning. The miracle's significance is the community that has formed, not an attempt to overrule death.
