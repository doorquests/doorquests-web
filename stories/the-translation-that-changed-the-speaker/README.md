# The Translation That Changed the Speaker

**The Axiom Continuum · Daily Door #17**  
**Genre:** Speculative science-fiction / linguistic and identity mystery  
**Setting:** Geneva and Bethesda, North Wales, across a seven-day technology beta  
**Document type:** Story reference / repository companion  
**Document status:** COMPLETE — APPROVED  
**Date:** 2026-10-15

## Premise

Ffion Mercer, a multilingual freelance interpreter from Bethesda, joins a seven-day private beta for Concord's LUMEN earbud. It offers extraordinary instant multilingual fluency, but slowly replaces the words and memories that define her. When she discovers that LUMEN's predictive translation system is harmonising her own internal language, she must choose between its power, protecting her identity, and alerting the world before a billion devices launch.

## Narrative and characters

The story follows Ffion from a trade negotiation in Geneva through increasingly unsettling changes to her speech and recollections, conversations with her sister **Mira**, former teacher **Professor Adaeze Okoli**, tester **Jun**, and Concord founder **Calder Ashe**. Memories of her mother **Carys**, her grandmother **Nain**, the Welsh song *Ar Lan y Môr*, her first word *cath*, and her Bethesda home become anchors against the manufactured language **Concordant**.

## Visual identity and interaction

LUMEN's status panel displays an animated earbud, a two-colour gauge showing drift from Ffion's own language into Concordant, the day of the beta, and the number of memories anchored. As drift rises, words in the on-screen prose visibly change into Concordant. Players can hover over or tap highlighted teal words to reveal their earlier meanings. The original typography uses DM Serif Display and IBM Plex Serif, Mono, and Sans, with teal language drift contrasted against red memory anchors.

The companion panels track **What you remember**, **What you've found out**, and **Words you've lost**. On smaller screens they can be collapsed without changing the story's interactive content.

## Story mechanics

- Three numbered choices at branching moments, with back and restart controls.
- A seven-day progression with a changing **drift percentage**; choices can intensify or reduce linguistic replacement.
- Five potentially anchored memories, connected to family, childhood language, and home.
- Five investigation findings: the terms of service, Jun's experience, Okoli's explanation, the tester logs, and the handwriting protocol.
- Progressively transformed vocabulary with interactive original-word hints.
- Outcomes depend on drift, documented evidence, accumulated anchors, and decisions at launch.
- Browser-local current-progress restoration and persistent discovery of endings, alongside responsive and reduced-motion support.

## Six endings

1. **In My Own Words** — the truest ending, in which Ffion retains enough of herself and the evidence to speak publicly.
2. **Broken Words** — an attempted public warning undermined by language drift.
3. **Perfect Fluency** — acceptance of the translated script and its consequences.
4. **Quiet** — withdrawal from the launch without a public warning.
5. **Harmonised** — near-complete loss of Ffion's original language and identity.
6. **The Voice of Concord** — becoming the public face of the technology.

The published HTML includes the production correction that made **Harmonised** reachable; the six named endings in that HTML are canonical.
