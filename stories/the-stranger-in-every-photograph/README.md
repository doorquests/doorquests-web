> The same stranger appears in family photographs spanning generations—including photographs of future events.

## Premise

In October 2026, **Mara**, thirty-eight, drives from Dublin to clear her late grandmother **Agnes Halloran's** house beside **Lough Carra**. Her husband **Joe** remains in Dublin with their eight-year-old daughter **Lily**. The cold house sends Mara to the hallway for Agnes's old grey coat, with its distinctive brass middle button; around Mara's neck is an oval locket Agnes gave her when she was six.

A hatbox holds generations of family photographs. In each one stands the same woman, always about forty, wearing a grey coat and positioned at the edge of the scene. A roll of film in Agnes's cellar darkroom bears a handwritten warning: **“Not yet.”** Developing it reveals pictures of events that have not happened, including Lily near the lake's dangerous jetty in August 2027.

The mystery concerns identity, time, family protection, and the difference between watching a tragedy and intervening in it.

## Characters

- **Mara:** Playable protagonist, mother of Lily and granddaughter of Agnes; recognizes that the stranger's features match her own.
- **Agnes Halloran:** Mara's grandmother, who kept the photographs and camera. She knew the mysterious woman as a child and left warnings about the red darkroom light.
- **Joe:** Mara's husband, concerned about her exhaustion and grief.
- **Lily:** Their eight-year-old daughter, eager to explore the jetty and confident she will be swimming by next summer.
- **Aunt Dot:** Agnes's younger sister, eighty-one; recalls the mysterious woman being called **the Watcher**.
- **Mr. Kemble:** Photographer at **Kemble & Sons** in Westport, who confirms the stranger appears on the original negative.
- **The woman in the grey coat:** The repeated figure whose identity is the story's central revelation.

## How to play

Each scene presents three numbered choices, selectable by clicking or pressing **1**, **2**, or **3**. **Retrace your last step** reverses a decision; **Start over** opens the hatbox again. The browser remembers discovered endings and saves in-progress play.

The story is built around close visual examination. **⌕ Under the loupe** magnifies a selected part of a photograph: a coat button, palm, locket, face, or another detail. **Turn it over** reveals pencilled inscriptions on the back of each print. Clues examined under the loupe are circled in **red grease pencil** when the player revisits the photograph.

## Six photographs

| Print | Date | Image and location | Stranger's presence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nora & Tom, St Brigid's** | **1931** | Great-grandmother Nora's wedding | By the railings, her face turned away |
| **Agnes, Harbour Road** | **1958** | Agnes, nineteen, near the **No. 14** bus | Holding Agnes back from boarding; the bus later goes off the quay |
| **Claire is 8!** | **1979** | Birthday party for Mara's mother | Behind the hedge, keeping the garden gate shut |
| **Mara & me, Lough Carra** | **1994** | Six-year-old Mara on the jetty with Agnes | On the opposite shore, facing the camera |
| **The lake** | **27 August 2027** | Lily at the edge of the jetty | Waist-deep in the lake, reaching toward Lily; nobody stands on the shore |
| **Tonight** | **2026** | Mara developing photographs in Agnes's darkroom | In the doorway behind Mara, holding a camera |

The first four images are inherited family prints; the latter two emerge from Agnes's previously undeveloped film.

## The Likeness: three identifying clues

The sidebar compares the stranger's attributes with Mara's:

1. **Grey wool coat, brass middle button:** Examine the stranger's coat in the **1931** wedding print. Mara is wearing the same coat.
2. **Oval locket:** Inspect the stranger's throat in the **1979** birthday photograph. The locket matches the one Agnes gave Mara.
3. **Diagonal scar across the left palm:** Look at the stranger's hand in the **1958** photograph and experience Mara cutting her own palm while replacing the cracked darkroom safelight bulb.

The identity score counts the third match only after both the stranger's scar has been examined and Mara has suffered her corresponding cut. The verdict progresses from **“A stranger.”** to **“One of three. Coincidence?”**, then **“Two of three. Go and look in the mirror.”** At the mirror Mara recognizes the woman as herself. **Two** matched clues, not necessarily all three, are enough to unlock the mirror scene.

## Album, darkroom, and clue records

**The Album** displays six photograph slots. At first, four historic prints are present and two spaces are marked as not yet developed. After development, the future images join them. When Mara identifies the woman, grease-pencil circles appear on the relevant album thumbnails.

**In Grease Pencil** records clues observed throughout the investigation, including the coat, locket, scar, Agnes's diary, and photographic evidence. **The Likeness** records matched features and current identity certainty.

The darkroom transforms the page to **safelight red**. Its broken bulb can scar Mara's palm; the developed roll exposes the future lake photograph. The red light also permits a journey into the **1958 photograph**, where Mara can prevent Agnes from boarding the bus, then return to her own time after forty seconds.

## The six canonical endings

| ID | Ending | Narrative outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **In the Picture** · *truest* | Recognize the stranger, enter 1958 and hold Agnes back from the bus, return, examine both future-lake details, and deliberately be present with Lily in 2027 |
| **E2** | **The Woman at the Edge** | Remain within the photographs indefinitely, watching over the family but abandoning Mara's present-day life |
| **E3** | **Ashes** | Burn the photographic evidence; the warning disappears, but the danger does not |
| **E4** | **A Rational Explanation** | Seek a medical explanation and leave the mystery unresolved even as the stranger appears in another photograph |
| **E5** | **Fogged** | Turn on white light in the darkroom, ruining the undeveloped images and their warnings |
| **E6** | **Left Unexplained** | Put away the hatbox and sell the house, choosing not to investigate |

### Exact strongest-ending dependency

The published HTML makes **In the Picture** available from the return scene (`ret`) only when the future roll has been developed (`seen.dev`) and **both** future-lake details have been inspected (`lakeFace` and `nobody`). That return scene is reached after Mara has followed the 1958 time-loop sequence. The final choice is to pin the lake photograph above Lily's calendar and **be on the shore** the following August. The story distinguishes closing the historic time loop from using that knowledge to protect Lily in person.

## Walkthrough to the truest ending — spoilers

1. Examine the **1931 wedding** and the stranger's **coat**, noting the brass button.
2. Examine the **1979 birthday photograph** and the stranger's **locket**. Two identity clues now match.
3. Go to the **hall mirror** and recognize the stranger as Mara.
4. In Agnes's **darkroom**, replace the cracked safelight bulb and cut Mara's palm.
5. **Develop the roll**. Examine the woman in the **2027 lake photograph** and look along the shore to confirm Mara is absent there.
6. With the relevant recognition and scar established, sit under the **red light** with the **1958 photograph**.
7. **Hold Agnes's arm**, keeping her away from the No. 14 bus. Say **“Not yet”** and return to the present.
8. After the forty-second return, choose to **pin the lake photograph above Lily's calendar** and resolve to be with her at the lake in August 2027.

The final sequence completes the old photographic loop without condemning Mara to exist forever at the edges of family photographs.

## Visual design and accessibility

The presentation uses a family-album palette, period-specific photographic filters, postcard-like prints, graphite inscriptions, and grease-pencil annotations. All six photographs are **inline SVG illustrations**; the loupe re-renders a selected crop of the same drawing rather than displaying unrelated images.

The fonts are **Libre Caslon Display**, **Libre Caslon Text**, **Space Mono**, and **Reenie Beanie**. Light and dark themes are supported, with a special darkroom-red state. The responsive layout becomes a single column below 900px, with mobile-safe spacing and collapsible reference panels in the reviewed HTML. Keyboard choices, back/restart controls, reduced-motion handling, and browser-local play restoration are provided.
