## Premise

For forty years, Halderstadt's Great Clock has lent hours from the future. At six in the evening, junior auditor Elsbeth Ruhm learns that the Clock will settle its accounts at midnight: twenty-four million hours divided among twenty-four thousand citizens. As Elsbeth investigates the Book of Hours, she discovers that wealthy borrowers transferred most of their obligations to the public. Her choices determine who pays, whether time itself survives, and whether the city keeps borrowing.

## Core mechanics

- Three numbered choices at each decision point, with back and restart controls.
- Simulated time advances from six o'clock toward midnight.
- A twenty-four-million-hour debt, of which eighteen million hours belong to the Merchants' Guild and Burgomaster.
- Correcting the Book with the clockmaker's ink returns that debt to its original borrowers.
- Three million hours voluntarily pledged by the poor quarter count twice under the Clock's Charter.
- A changing per-person debt indicator, ledger, and seven discovered notes reflect player choices.
- Six endings, browser-local progress restoration, persistent ending discoveries, and a compact mobile ledger.
