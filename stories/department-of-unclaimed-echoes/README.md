# Department of Unclaimed Echoes

**The Axiom Continuum — Daily Door #31**  
**Genre:** Speculative comedy / satire / supernatural bureaucracy  
**Setting:** Bureau of Posthumous Affairs, Floor 4, during a workday from 09:00 to 17:00  
**Estimated play time:** 10–15 minutes per run (source estimate)  
**Documentation status:** Complete; Founder play review pending  
**Date:** 2026-10-15

> An afterlife bureaucracy struggles with forgotten promises and a misplaced declaration of love.

## Premise

You are a **Junior Clerk, Grade VII**, working in the **Department of Unclaimed Echoes**, Floor 4 of the **Bureau of Posthumous Affairs**. The Department handles words that were spoken but never received: apologies left on answering machines, goodbyes lost as trains departed, and things whispered beside graves. These are called *echoes*. Millions arrive in pneumatic tubes and are stored until someone can deliver them—or the shredder destroys them.

A memo on your desk identifies **Echo 1987-03-14-0412**, filed under **COMPLAINTS (NOISE)**, as due for destruction at **17:00 today** under the Department's thirty-nine-year retention procedure. Its entire content is only four words, or almost four. A previous clerk has written in the margin: **“this isn't a complaint.”**

The player must decide whether to follow procedure or risk time, reputation and reassignment to help two people finish a conversation interrupted thirty-nine years ago.

## How to play

Choices are labelled **A**, **B** and **C**. Select them with mouse or touch, or press keyboard **1**, **2** or **3**. Available options display any **time costs**, such as an additional thirty minutes, and any **audit risk** they incur.

**Retrace your last step** restores the previous game state. **Start over** takes a new ticket and returns to the opening desk scene. Previously discovered endings are recorded locally in the browser, and the reviewed website edition also stores the current scene and undo history.

The clock begins at **09:00**. Each full lift journey, appointment, form or errand can move the clock toward the **17:00 shredder deadline**. Careless shortcuts risk administrative punishment; elaborate formal procedures can use too much time.

## Signature interactive systems

### Departmental board

The heading contains three continuously updated departmental instruments:

- **NOW SERVING:** A take-a-number display, beginning with **4,117,902**, that advances with game actions.
- **WALL CLOCK:** An analogue clock and digital readout counting through the day toward **17:00**. It warns of the impending shredder deadline.
- **ECHO 0412:** A green waveform showing the declaration's remaining strength. It dims as time expires; when Margaret's missing reply is discovered, a second golden trace appears.

### Form 77-B — the Docket

The original file is misclassified. To reclassify it for delivery, the clerk needs three stamps:

| Stamp | Department | Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Records** | Mr. Fennimore | Floor 2 |
| **Sentiment Compliance** | Ms. Vane | Floor 7 |
| **Delivery Authorisation** | Mr. Thrum | Floor 9 |

Proper stamps read **APPROVED**, while forged examples appear in dashed blue and carry the deliberately misspelled **APPRVOED** stamp. The distinction matters: forging **Records** causes a misdelivery, while forged **Sentiment Compliance** cannot qualify for the strongest ending.

### Working hours and lifts

The working day advances in **thirty-minute units**, beginning at 09:00 and ending at 17:00. The Bureau's wood-panelled lifts take **thirty minutes per trip**, playing the same bar of music on an endless loop.

The handbook's conventional procedures are intentionally slow: Records' **Form 9** takes **ninety minutes**, and a full Sincerity Audit takes **two hours**. The puzzle rewards finding legitimate, compassionate alternatives rather than relying on fraudulent shortcuts.

### Audit Risk

Forging a stamp or tampering with the sincerity meter adds an **audit-risk mark**. The Department's policy is that **three procedural irregularities** lead to reassignment to the **Department of Hold Music**.

The Audit Risk panel shows the current total. Forgery may cause additional consequences even before three marks: in particular, a false Records stamp makes the recipient's address unreliable.

### Forgotten promises — the In-Tray

Three smaller obligations wait beside the main file:

| Promise | Original context | Human resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **“I'll call you tomorrow.”** | Dennis Achterberg to his brother Paul, 2003 | Paul's granddaughter finds an old phone book with his number underlined |
| **“I'll fix that shelf this weekend.”** | Ray Oduya to his wife Bisi, 1996 | A grandson discovers Ray's level and an apologetic note |
| **“Back in five minutes.”** | A Hove bicycle-shop sign, 1979 | The abandoned sign finds a new home in another shop window |

Processing a promise normally consumes **thirty minutes**. **Mrs. Gristle** requires at least one promise to be processed before she countersigns a delivery. In the current HTML, if the player reaches the delivery scene without doing this, the required processing is applied to the departing action: the extra half-hour is charged and the **“I'll call you tomorrow”** item is recorded as processed.

This is part of the story's theme: **little promises first**.

## People and departments

### Floor 4 — Mrs. Gristle and the Handbook

**Mrs. Gristle**, the Senior Clerk, has served since the Department used quills. She warns that Form 77-B needs three stamps, and hints that Floor 9 has an **Unsorted shelf** for echoes that arrived at the same instant.

The Department Handbook provides rules for reclassification, accepted delivery methods, and audit penalties.

### Waiting Room 6 — Walter Quince

**Walter Quince** died on **16 March 1987** and has waited thirty-nine years in a beige room, wearing a 1980s overcoat and holding a **British Rail ticket from Crewe to Euston**.

He recalls calling his wife Margaret from the platform and saying he loved her before the connection cut off. What troubles him is that he believes she started to say something back. The player may ask Walter to **testify to his sincerity**, a key legitimate shortcut.

### Floor 2 — Records and Mr. Fennimore

**Mr. Fennimore** demands a lengthy Form 9, unless the player takes the risk of forging a stamp. A more humane alternative is to ask about a brass echo-cartridge addressed to Fennimore himself, which regulations prevent him from opening.

When the player opens it, Fennimore hears an affirmation spoken at his birth. In gratitude, he **stamps the reclassification form legitimately**, checks the address and adds an expedite note.

### Floor 7 — Sentiment Compliance and Ms. Vane

**Ms. Vane** oversees a sincerity meter. Options include a two-hour formal review, having Walter testify, playing the short echo for her, or tampering with the meter.

Walter's testimony pushes the needle beyond **SINCERE** and **PROFOUND** to an exceptional reading of **JUST SO**. Playing the echo itself offers a second, more compact route to a legitimate stamp.

### Floor 9 — Delivery Authorisation and Mr. Thrum

**Mr. Thrum**, Director of Deliveries, authorises a delivery when the two preliminary stamps are present and the proper method has been chosen. Beyond his office lies the **Unsorted shelf**.

An hour spent searching the shelf reveals the response to Walter's message: **Echo 1987-03-14-0413**, recorded at the same second. There is no prescribed form for delivering an echo to the dead—but no express rule against doing so, either.

### Delivery methods — Handbook §12

The living may receive an echo through:

- **Form 12-D — Dream:** Margaret hears Walter's voice in her sleep.
- **Form 12-S — Song:** A tune from their 1951 dance hall plays on the care home's radio.
- **Form 12-C — Coincidence:** Margaret unexpectedly finds something connected to Walter.

Direct delivery, described as **“a voice in the room,”** was discontinued in 1888 following complaints. Any of the three approved methods can support the strongest ending.

## The three important echoes — spoilers

### Echo 1987-03-14-0412

**Source:** Payphone, Platform 4, Crewe Station  
**Time:** 21:47, 14 March 1987  
**Sender:** Walter Quince  
**Recipient:** Margaret Quince, now ninety-one, at Larkhill House, Room 12

> “Margaret — the train's in, I've got to — I love y—” [click]

### Echo 1987-03-14-0413

**Source:** Telephone, 14 Albany Road, Stoke  
**Time:** The same second as Echo 0412  
**Speaker:** Margaret Quince

> “— I love you too, Walt, you daft —” [click]

Margaret's answer was sent into the dead connection. It remained stuck to Walter's echo on the **Unsorted shelf** for thirty-nine years. The strongest ending requires the player to locate this cartridge and personally bring it to Walter.

### Echo 1961-09-02-7730

**Source:** Maternity Ward, Leeds General  
**Recipient:** Mr. Fennimore

An affectionate message to him as a newborn says how proud the speaker already is of the careful person he will become. Helping him hear this echo opens one of the strongest legitimate administrative shortcuts.

## Six canonical endings

| ID | Ending | Narrative result |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **Both Ways** · *truest* | Walter's message reaches Margaret, and her reply reaches Walter. The necessary stamps are legitimate and delivery is completed in time. |
| **E2** | **One Way** | Margaret receives Walter's echo, but his wait remains unresolved because he never hears hers. This also applies when forged Sentiment approval disqualifies an otherwise complete two-way route. |
| **E3** | **Shredded at Five** | The clock expires before the procedure can be completed, and the echo is destroyed. |
| **E4** | **Hold Music** | Three audit irregularities trigger the clerk's reassignment and leave the main echo to the shredder. |
| **E5** | **Approved for Destruction** | The player authorises the original Form 404 and declines to pursue the case. |
| **E6** | **Misdelivered** | A forged Records stamp sends the declaration to **Margaret Quin**, one N, a thirty-four-year-old dental hygienist unrelated to Walter. |

### Exact end-of-case logic

In the **current committed HTML**, closing out the delivery evaluates its result this way:

```js
function endFor(s, both) {
  if (s.forged.includes("rec")) return "E6";
  // The strongest ending requires genuine Records and Sentiment stamps.
  if (both && s.forged.length) return "E2";
  return both ? "E1" : "E2";
}
```

This resolves an earlier inconsistency: a forged **Sentiment Compliance** stamp no longer unlocks **Both Ways**. The narrative continues to distinguish the stricter ideal of legitimate approvals from the available, risky shortcut routes.

When Margaret's reply is missing, simply closing the delivery produces **One Way**. In the corrected edition, the E2 narration distinguishes between an undiscovered reply, one replaced on the shelf and one retained by the clerk rather than delivered.

The time and audit systems can override attempted progress: an active route at or after **17:00** ends in **Shredded at Five**, while reaching the audit threshold can trigger **Hold Music**.

## Walkthrough to the truest ending — spoilers

1. **09:00:** Pull **Echo 0412** from the file and listen to it.
2. Visit **Walter in Waiting Room 6**. Ask whether he would testify to the sincerity of his words.
3. Take the lift to **Records, Floor 2**. Ask Mr. Fennimore about his personal echo and **open it for him**. He gives the first **legitimate stamp** and verifies Margaret's address. Ask him about the Unsorted shelf if desired.
4. Visit **Sentiment Compliance, Floor 7**. Bring Walter to testify; Ms. Vane issues the second **legitimate stamp**.
5. Go to **Floor 9** and search the **Unsorted shelf**. Find **Echo 0413**, Margaret's response.
6. Present the form to **Mr. Thrum**, obtain the final Delivery Authorisation stamp and choose **Dream**, **Song** or **Coincidence**.
7. Ensure at least one forgotten promise is processed; if the In-Tray is still untouched, Mrs. Gristle's mandatory processing adds time and records the first promise at the final action.
8. **Personally bring Echo 0413 to Walter** in Waiting Room 6 before the shredder deadline.

With the helpful shortcuts, the clerk can finish well before five without forging a stamp.

**Meaning of the strongest ending:** Margaret receives the words she thought had been lost. Walter hears what Margaret answered, finally relinquishes his decades-old train ticket and passes through the waiting-room door. The two echoes have been delivered **both ways**.

## Interface and art direction

The design is bureaucratic satire rendered in **municipal green**, **rubber-stamp red**, **carbon-paper blue**, amber and off-white form paper.

Typography uses **Big Shoulders Display** for signage, **Libre Franklin** for narrative prose and **Courier Prime** for memos, forms and ticket displays. The interface includes a live-updating analogue clock and counter, document stamps, an echo waveform, responsive layouts and light/dark themes.

For mobile, the reviewed edition places **The Docket**, **Audit Risk**, **Your In-Tray** and **Found in the Files** in four collapsible reference sections. Keyboard shortcuts, undo and progress restoration support repeat exploration.

### Rendering hardening in the latest HTML

A post-publication rendering issue caused CSS rules to appear as page text and allowed the clock artwork to grow dramatically. The Founder obtained a revised version from Claude, **replaced the canonical HTML directly**, and confirmed that the replacement displayed correctly.

The working revision avoids relying on an ordinary inline `<style>` element being preserved by the viewing environment: **the page's own JavaScript inserts the CSS at runtime**. The analogue clock and waveform also have independent size safeguards, and a notice is shown if JavaScript is unavailable. These protections are specific to the corrected canonical HTML rather than claims about every browser-based viewer.

This README records the **working repository edition**, not the broken earlier deployment. Neither the issue nor the fix should be taken to imply that all other Daily Doors need the same modification.

## Quality assurance and scope

The source file `unclaimed-echoes.md` supplied the original premise, handbook rules, floor descriptions, three echo texts, six endings, strongest-ending walkthrough, interface design and episode ideas, all preserved here.

The source Markdown describes an **exhaustive** audit of more than 500,000 story states. That claim is **not established** by the available final validation record. The Story #31 review ran a **bounded state exploration**, rather than enumerating every possible repeated lift or department visit. In the final review, all six endings were reached and no invalid scene destinations were identified in the examined transitions.

The current HTML includes subsequent corrections to the Sentiment forgery outcome, mandatory promise-processing state and One Way narration, plus the Founder-tested runtime-style rendering fix. Code-level validation and Founder's successful render test do not amount to exhaustive browser/device play testing.
