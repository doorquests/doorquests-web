The Uncatalogued Room

Every century has a story. Every discovery opens a door.

A storm in the night toppled a storeroom shelf. Twelve objects lost their catalogue labels, and the gallery opens at dawn. Examine each object, open its evidence drawer, and write a catalogue card that says where it came from, when, what it was for — and how sure you can be.

“Nothing goes on display unless we can explain how we know.”

The storm, the storeroom and the curator are fictional framing. The objects are original illustrations modelled on documented museum objects or object types; every historical statement links to its sources in .

A note from the curator

The shelf came down at two in the morning. The objects are safe — the labels are not. The gallery opens at dawn, and nothing goes on display unless we can explain how we know.

For each object: examine it, open the evidence drawer, and fill in the catalogue card — place, period and function. Cite the evidence for every claim, and say how sure you are: Established, Probable or Uncertain. I will return any card that claims more than its evidence allows. An honest “uncertain” is not a failure.

— The Curator

How to read the labels

Established

Supported by direct, widely accepted evidence.

Probable

Best-supported reading, but resting on inference, style or indirect evidence.

Uncertain

Competing interpretations or too little evidence to choose.

Fictional framing

The storm, curator, storeroom notes and teaching scenarios. Never evidence about real history.

How it works

Catalogue an object

  1. Examine the drawing. Gold circles (and the “Details” buttons under the drawing) open the matching evidence card.
  2. Read the evidence drawer. Cards are labelled: observation, museum record, reference, comparison, lab result — or fictional framing and teaching scenarios.
  3. Fill in the card. For place, period and function, choose an answer, a confidence level, and the evidence you are citing.
  4. Submit to the curator. Each field is checked: is the answer defensible, does the cited evidence actually bear on it and support it, and is your confidence right?

Confidence

Established direct, widely accepted evidence. Probable the best-supported reading, but inferred. Uncertain competing readings or too little evidence. Claiming too much is returned, just like a wrong answer. Claiming too little is returned too, more gently.

Restored and verified

An object is restored once its card is accepted. It is also verified if it was accepted with no more than two returned cards in that attempt. Verified records earn Keyring marks. You can re-examine any object from scratch.

Keyboard

Everything works with Tab, Shift+Tab, Space and Enter. Radio groups use the arrow keys. There are no timers.
