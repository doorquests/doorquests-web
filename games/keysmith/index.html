Start a shift

  • Locks ride the conveyor toward the test station. Each shows its pin depths.
  • Select a notch and file it down to the lock's depth. Filing only removes metal; it can never be undone.
  • Cut past the red line and the key is ruined at once. Too shallow and the lock will not turn.
  • Press Test key when ready. If the lock reaches the station first, the key is tested as it is.
  • Three spoiled keys end a ranked shift. Perfect keys in a row build a streak multiplier, up to ×2.

How to play

The job

Each lock on the conveyor has a row of pins. The number under each pin is how deep that notch must be, in filing units. Your blank key starts with every notch at 0.

Filing

  • Drag sideways across the key on the workbench to stroke the file. Each stroke length bites one unit.
  • Hold to file (button, or hold Space): one unit straight away, then — after a short pause — 10 units per second.
  • Fine cut (button or F): exactly one unit, for finishing.
  • The file bites at most one unit every 1/20 s. Faster strokes skate without cutting. Every method uses the same rules.
  • Pressing on the key without moving only selects a notch. Nothing is cut by accident.

The lines on each notch

The dashed brass line is the target. The green band is the tolerance that still opens the lock. The red line is where the key is ruined. The table under the workbench lists every number.

If the lock reaches the test station before you press Test key, your key is tested exactly as it is. If it opens, it counts, with no time bonus.

Score

  • Opened key: 100 + accuracy (up to 100) + time left (up to 100). Perfect keys add 50.
  • Accuracy averages each notch: 1 − |error| ÷ 3, never below 0.
  • Streak: each perfect key in a row raises the multiplier on the next perfect key by ×0.25, up to ×2.00. Any key that is not perfect resets it.
  • Completing a ranked shift adds 150 per key you did not spoil.

Tiers

  • Bronze — Apprentice: 10 locks, 3–4 pins, depths 4–16, tolerance ±2, perfect within 1.
  • Silver — Journeyman: 12 locks, 4–6 pins, depths 4–20, tolerance ±1, perfect means exact. Curved blanks appear: their tip notches are hardened and file at half speed (hatched).
  • Gold — Master Keysmith: 12 locks, 5–7 pins, depths 4–24, no overcut allowed (1 under is fine), perfect means exact. Curved and skeleton blanks; a skeleton key must be cut exactly.

Finishing a ranked shift with fewer than three spoiled keys earns that tier's seal for the Gamortal Keyring. Practice and the tutorial never affect records.

Pausing

P or Pause stops the conveyor. Switching to another tab or app pauses automatically. Your shift is saved and can be continued after a reload.

Keysmith is an arcade abstraction. It is not a lock-cutting specification and it teaches nothing about copying or bypassing real locks.
