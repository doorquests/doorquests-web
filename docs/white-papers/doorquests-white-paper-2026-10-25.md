# Executive Summary

DoorQuests is an emerging browser-first universe of interactive experiences organized around one intuitive gesture: **choose a door**. Behind that door may be a branching story, an evidence-based mystery, a logic challenge, a replayable game, an exploratory simulation, or—eventually—a technically realistic problem demanding diagnosis and repair. The doorway is at once a narrative motif, a navigation pattern, and a common language linking distinct kinds of play and learning.

The project was launched on **October 16, 2026**, under the creative umbrella of **The Axiom Continuum**. In its first nine days, its published footprint expanded from the initial interactive stories to an extensive foundational entertainment catalog and the opening of an educational Discovery Wing. As of this paper's snapshot, the core entertainment program encompasses **31 Doortals (stories), 31 Cluortals (cases), 31 Chaortals (challenges), and four officially published Gamortals (games)**. The original inaugural slate includes 31 Gamortal concepts; the first four games have been published, with **#005, The Daily Delve**, at the prototype-design stage. An initial set of Discovery Wing collection examples has also been developed. These figures describe program milestones, not measured audience demand or independent commercial traction.

The strategic horizon has three distinct, complementary propositions:

1. **Play** — compelling entertainment through stories, cases, challenges, and skill-based games.
2. **Discover** — hands-on exploration of academic disciplines and knowledge systems through the **Discortal Discovery Wing**.
3. **Practice** — simulated diagnosis, construction, repair, operation, and technical decision-making through a proposed future applied-professions destination provisionally titled **The Guilds**.

Crucially, these phases are **not synonymous with release authorization**. The entertainment catalog contains live products and approved concepts; the educational expansion includes released examples and future subjects; the proposed vocational arm is conceptual only. The project is deliberately founder-governed: prototypes are audited, playtested, approved individually, and only then added to public catalogs. The immediate development priority remains **Gamortals #005–#031**.

**Strategic thesis:** A consistent brand and low-friction, self-contained browser delivery system can support many kinds of interactive experiences without collapsing them into one genre. DoorQuests can be a place visitors return to because there is always another meaningful door to open—not because they are forced into accounts, streak obligations, or complex software installation.

# 1. Origins and Founding Thesis

## 1.1 From a story experiment to an experience architecture

DoorQuests began as a creative experiment in interactive storytelling. The original question—*what if every door opened to a different experience?*—quickly extended beyond branching fiction. Mystery cases introduced investigation; interactive challenges introduced procedural reasoning; original games introduced reflexes, strategy, simulation, scores, and replay. In parallel, the larger Axiom Continuum creative framework made room for experiences whose chief purpose is discovery rather than entertainment.

The project's rapid initial build is an important origin story, but not its sole measure of progress. A large catalog creates value only if each door delivers a worthwhile experience, the navigation remains understandable, and releases stay technically reliable.

## 1.2 The door as an organizing principle

A door is legible across age groups, cultures, and subject matter. In DoorQuests it conveys four things:

- **Choice:** the visitor controls where to go.
- **Uncertainty:** the full experience is not known before entry.
- **Threshold:** crossing changes the context or rules of engagement.
- **Agency:** outcomes emerge from decisions, observation, and action.

The system should preserve that metaphor without making every experience mechanically identical. The point is a shared entry language, not creative uniformity.

## 1.3 Relationship to The Axiom Continuum

**DoorQuests** is the destination brand. **The Axiom Continuum** is credited as its creative origin. The umbrella may support other creative experiments, but this white paper concerns the DoorQuests universe and its directly related roadmap. A common creative parent does not mean every experimental concept is part of the published DoorQuests catalog.

# 2. Current Product Architecture

## 2.1 Four founding entertainment collections

| Collection | Experience | Core visitor action | Status at snapshot |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Doortals** | Branching stories | **Choose** a path and shape an outcome | 31 published |
| **Cluortals** | Interactive cases | **Investigate** evidence and find the truth | 31 published |
| **Chaortals** | Puzzles and challenges | **Reason**, experiment, and unlock a solution | 31 published |
| **Gamortals** | Original browser games | **Play**, master systems, and pursue results | 4 published; 31-concept inaugural slate |

These four collections form the public entertainment foundation. Their mechanics, intended session lengths, scoring approaches, and accessibility needs vary substantially. The common denominator is a recognizable DoorQuests visual and navigation identity.

### Doortals — Choose your path

A Doortal invites visitors into an authored world where choices shape the progression and outcome. The initial 31-story catalog established the daily-door idea and a visual gallery of luminous arches. The format favors narrative consequence, exploration, multiple routes, and replay for alternative endings.

### Cluortals — Find the truth

A Cluortal is an evidence-oriented case. Visitors examine records, interrogate claims, reconstruct timelines, and determine what happened. The collection's darker, case-specific door language distinguishes investigation from fiction while retaining a common world. Success should depend on justified inference, not arbitrary guessing.

### Chaortals — Unlock the impossible

A Chaortal is a self-contained reasoning or procedural challenge, typically supporting differentiated difficulty. Challenges can draw on logic, arithmetic, mechanisms, spatial reasoning, cryptography, and other formal systems. The 31 published titles establish a substantial catalog, but each experience must still be individually accurate and solvable. Bronze, Silver, and Gold are recognizable difficulty concepts; their criteria must be explicit within each challenge.

### Gamortals — Play your way

Gamortals focus on mechanics, competition with oneself, simulation, scores, and repeat sessions. Their inaugural concept slate includes 31 proposed games. The first releases show intentional breadth:

| # | Game | Genre / distinctive loop | Publication status |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **001** | **Last Candle** | Arcade survival; balance a candle's environment through doors and windows | **Officially published** |
| **002** | **Keysmith** | Precision arcade; interact with keys and timing | **Officially published** |
| **003** | **Holes Between Rooms** | Mini golf through portals between rooms | **Officially published** |
| **004** | **Threshold** | Rhythm arcade; match geometric openings and cross on the beat | **Officially published** |
| **005** | **The Daily Delve** | Ten-floor, door-choice roguelite with a shared daily dungeon seed | **Design / prototype prompt prepared; not published** |

**The Endless Hall** remains a proposed longer-term flagship, not a released game. Other items in the 31-game slate remain concepts until their individual development, audit, and founder approvals.

## 2.2 The guide and discovery of content

**Wortal, the Warden of the Doors**, provides the **Find My Door** recommendation experience. It offers a simple visitor-facing route into stories, mysteries, challenges, games, or discovery. The classic and enhanced homepage implementations were updated to distinguish an explicit **Game** choice and recommend published Gamortals, rather than folding games into the challenge category.

This is more than a navigation improvement. As the universe grows, visitors will need a trustworthy guide that recognizes content type, interaction preference, and mood. Any recommendation engine should select only genuinely available experiences and explain what it recommends without forcing a visitor's choice.

**Surprise Me** offers another interaction model: deliberate serendipity. It should continue to link only to existing experiences and preserve visitor agency.

## 2.3 Other emerging entertainment destinations

The project also developed a **Tomortal** gateway beyond the four founding collections. Ideas include:

- **Sonortals:** audio-first listening experiences, soundscapes, and auditory investigation.
- **Dilemmortals:** value conflicts, ethical tradeoffs, and consequential decisions without necessarily one correct answer.
- **Mystortals:** deliberately concealed experience types; a door may change genre after entry.
- **Vaults:** a prospective archive of artifacts, lore, documents, and connections within the creative universe.

These concepts should be governed as distinct formats. Their presence in a conceptual architecture does not imply that an entire numbered slate has been built.

# 3. The Discovery Wing — Discortal

## 3.1 Purpose and identity

**Discortal** is the DoorQuests Discovery Wing: a place where curiosity leads to interactive learning. The associated **DoortalPortal** name and domain can support discovery identity while routing visitors to DoorQuests. The wing is meant to emphasize investigation, manipulation of evidence, experimentation, comparison, and defensible conclusions—not merely educational text decorated with buttons.

A visitor should learn by taking an action inside a simulated system and understanding its consequences. The format should communicate uncertainties and assumptions clearly, especially for historical, scientific, social, and philosophical subjects.

## 3.2 Existing subject foundation

The established Discovery Wing subjects include **Geortals, Hisortals, Mathortals, Sciortals, Biortals, Artortals, Langortals, Musicortals, Comportals, AIortals, STEMortals, Mythortals, Culinortals, Philosortals, and Econortals**. Individual introductory experiences have been developed across these subjects; this document does not claim that 31 entries per discipline have been published.

Representative introductory concepts and examples include:

|:--|:--|:--|
| Hisortals | **The Uncatalogued Room** | Catalogue artifacts from historical evidence and state confidence |
| STEMortals | **The Lost Drawings of Kestrel Gorge** | Reconstruct bridge structures and evaluate engineering constraints |

| Econortals | **The Price of Bread** | Simulate market supply, demand, shortages, and household tradeoffs |

These examples illustrate the philosophy of Discortal: the lesson arises through interaction, examination, or defensible choice. Exact publication status is tracked per experience in the live site and repository, rather than inferred from inclusion in this paper.

Collection clarity depends on boundaries:

- **Mathortals / Statortals / Calcortals:** broad mathematics / statistical reasoning and evidence / change and accumulation.
- **Econortals / Finortals:** economic systems and markets / personal and institutional finance decisions.
- **Civortals / Poliortals:** government structure and civic institutions / political coalition-building and negotiation.
- **Hisortals / Cultortals:** historical evidence and change over time / cultures and the transmission of practices.
- **Langortals / Lexortals:** language systems and grammar / literary interpretation, vocabulary, and translation.
- **Sciortals / Chemortals / Medortals:** general scientific inquiry / chemistry / health and medical evidence.

Disciplines can overlap productively when each experience has a clear primary teaching goal and does not duplicate another collection's central mechanic.

# 4. Phase III Vision — The Guilds

## 4.1 A third proposition: practice

A proposed **vocational and applied-technical destination** would extend DoorQuests beyond entertainment and academic discovery. The preferred working identity is **The Guilds**, with “The Vocational Wing” and “The Technic Wing” retained as alternative naming concepts.

Its proposition is distinct:

> **Learn the system. Find the fault. Master the craft.**

Where Discortal explores why a principle works, a Guild simulation asks visitors to diagnose and act within a modeled system. The experience has to reward correct reasoning, appropriate precautions, efficient use of tests, and verification of outcomes.

**This is a future concept only.** No site-wide Guild launch or set of published Guild collections is authorized by this paper.

## 4.2 Proposed professional disciplines

A prospective slate of 15 applied disciplines provides exploration breadth without promising a fixed release count:

| Working collection | Professional field | Example interactive task |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Electortals | Electrical trades | Diagnose a virtual circuit fault and verify safe restoration |
| Plumbortals | Plumbing | Isolate pressure losses and valve faults |
| Mechortals | Mechanical maintenance | Inspect bearings, gears, and power transmission |
| Autortals | Automotive technology | Diagnose a vehicle that cranks but will not start |
| Buildortals | Construction | Plan a structure around loads, materials, and constraints |
| HVACortals | Heating and cooling | Diagnose airflow and thermal-control failures |
| Weldortals | Welding | Select joints and inspect simulated defects |
| Aviortals | Aviation maintenance | Follow a modeled troubleshooting procedure |
| Marineortals | Marine systems | Restore a simulated propulsion or auxiliary subsystem |
| Robortals | Robotics | Diagnose sensors and actuators in automation |
| Netortals | Network technology | Trace configuration and connectivity faults |
| Cyberortals | Cybersecurity | Investigate and contain a fictional defensive incident |
| Agrortals | Agriculture and equipment | Diagnose irrigation and equipment problems |
| Surveyortals | Surveying and geomatics | Reconcile measurement errors and spatial boundaries |
| Fabricortals | Machining and fabrication | Produce parts within simulated tolerance constraints |

The naming list is exploratory and may require significant refinement. Similarities to existing terminology, confusion between terms, and possible brand conflicts must be evaluated before adoption.

## 4.3 The Guild mastery loop

A strong vocational simulator can follow a five-part cycle:

1. **Inspect:** examine equipment, logs, symptoms, and conditions.
2. **Diagnose:** formulate and test competing explanations.
3. **Repair or act:** choose a justified intervention within the simulator.
4. **Verify:** test whether the intended condition was actually restored.
5. **Master:** receive feedback on correctness, safety, efficiency, and uncertainty.

This structure makes a Guild simulation fundamentally different from a trivia quiz. A correct answer entered without identifying the problem or verifying the result should not earn the same outcome as a careful, well-supported repair.

## 4.4 Boundaries for safety and accuracy

The Guilds must maintain explicit safety distinctions. Electrical, aviation, medical, welding, machinery, and other specialist systems can be hazardous in real life. Simulations should use fictional or constrained environments, model safe practices, and explain that the experience is **not certification, licensure, professional instruction, or authorization to perform hazardous work**. Content should avoid presenting unverified or incomplete repair instructions as operationally safe. Any technical claims should undergo subject-specific expert validation before public representation as educational guidance.

The same principle applies to medical subject matter in Discortal: interactive fictional diagnostics can teach reasoning, but they must not pose as patient-specific medical advice.

# 5. Experience Design Principles

## 5.1 Interaction before exposition

Good DoorQuests experiences make the user *do* something: choose, inspect, test, estimate, assemble, listen, time, compare, or defend a conclusion. Explanatory text is useful when it supports an action and responds to the learner's decisions. It should not obscure the interactive task.

## 5.2 Legible, consequential choices

A door choice should have a visible stake. Visitors need enough information to formulate an expectation even when the outcome remains uncertain. Arbitrary surprises can support fiction, but should be used sparingly in knowledge or systems-based experiences where users expect reasoned causality.

## 5.3 Different scoring regimes for different purposes

A competitive arcade game may reward precision and high scores. A historical inquiry may reward calibrated confidence. An ethical dilemma may require reflection rather than a single gold-medal answer. The user interface should make the rules explicit and should never imply that all experiences produce the same kind of truth or accomplishment.

## 5.4 Accessible entry, layered mastery

The user should understand an experience's basic loop quickly. Bronze, Silver, and Gold provide a useful recurring convention where difficulty tiers make sense; accessibility accommodations must not be treated as lesser intellectual achievement. Each experience should support appropriate keyboard, touch, contrast, focus, motion, and audio alternatives.

## 5.5 No false claims of evidence or certainty

When an experience uses real-world subject matter, it must distinguish established information from hypotheses, interpretations, and dramatic invention. The system should reward confidence calibrated to evidence. For cultural, political, or religious materials, represent multiple perspectives fairly and avoid reducing real groups to stereotypes or universal assumptions.

## 5.6 Browser-first and privacy-conscious

The preferred default is an immediately playable browser experience with no signup. Local storage can retain preferences and progress. Site analytics, where deployed, should be disclosed appropriately through the project's privacy documentation. No account-free, browser-only mechanism should be described as a secure, tamper-proof leaderboard.

# 6. Technical Architecture and Distribution

## 6.1 Domain and site organization

The public identity centers on **https://doorquests.com/**. Existing paths organize experiences by collection: stories, cases, challenges, games, the Tomortal gateway, and the Discovery Wing. A future vocational destination might use `/guilds/`, but the path and public product naming remain unapproved.

The related **doortalportal.com** domain has been associated with the Discovery Wing and is intended to remain directed toward DoorQuests until a separate identity is warranted. Domain ownership, hosting behavior, and publication readiness should be checked when any expansion is authorized.

## 6.2 Repository and release practice

The principal website repository is **`doorquests/doorquests-web`**, using its `main` branch for current publication. Operational working convention favors **one changed file per commit**, making each modification traceable and reversible. The repository, rather than an informal conversation summary, is the source of truth for current deployed page content.

A repeatable release pipeline is:

**Concept → independent review → complete prototype → technical audit → unlisted live preview → founder playtest → explicit approval → official publication → catalog and homepage update → verification / journal.**

The distinction between **prototype** and **official** is essential. An uploaded, runnable game is not necessarily approved for public promotion. An individual concept approval is not permission to publish or change unrelated pages.

## 6.3 Standalone artifacts

Many experiences are implemented as a self-contained HTML5 file with embedded CSS and JavaScript. This architecture has practical advantages: straightforward auditing, direct links, minimal build dependencies, and easier preservation of a specific interactive release. It also carries maintenance challenges if common navigation, accessibility fixes, or analytics policies must be propagated across many pages.

Future refactoring could introduce carefully governed shared components or lightweight registries. Such refactoring should be incremental and tested against each existing experience rather than undertaken as a disruptive platform rewrite.

## 6.4 Persistence and the Keyring

The **Keyring** is the project's broader vocabulary for accomplishments and unlocked progress. Some games maintain their own namespaced browser records. Those game-local records must not be falsely presented as synchronized with a shared Keyring unless integration has been implemented and verified.

A later canonical achievement model could define stable identifiers, versioned saves, migration rules, import/export behavior, and privacy boundaries. Until then, each game's published mechanics and persistence behavior should be documented truthfully.

## 6.5 Daily experiences and deterministic seeds

Daily features should avoid accidental divergence between visitors. **The Daily Delve** proposes a UTC-date-based daily seed, stable encounter generation, and shareable outcomes without accounts. Its design must ensure that one player's choices do not shift another player's ostensibly identical dungeon. A local-device clock and localStorage cannot provide verified competition; this limitation should be explicit, not hidden.

# 7. Content Quality, Evidence, and Safety Governance

## 7.1 Founder-governed status model

Suggested canonical status categories:

| Status | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| **Proposed** | Idea identified; no development or publication commitment |
| **Concept approved** | Scope accepted for further design; not released |
| **Prototype in development** | Artifact under construction |
| **Ready for founder playtest** | Initial audit permits an unlisted preview; not official |
| **Founder approved** | Founder has playtested and authorized publication |
| **Officially published** | Live public entry and relevant catalog integration verified |
| **Under revision / withdrawn** | Existing content requires correction or is no longer promoted |

A status should reflect what has actually happened, not what is expected to happen next.

## 7.2 Audit disciplines

Different content types require different tests:

- **Narrative:** path completion, branch logic, endings, continuity, and responsive presentation.
- **Mystery:** evidence sufficiency, consistent timelines, clue fairness, and defensible conclusions.
- **Challenge:** solvability, scoring integrity, calibrated difficulty, and absence of accidental shortcuts.
- **Game:** valid state transitions, deterministic scoring, input fairness, save recovery, audio/motion behavior, and replayability.
- **Discovery:** correctness of educational models, uncertainty calibration, and explanatory evidence.
- **Guild simulation:** realistic state behavior, domain verification, safe constraints, and appropriate disclaimers.

A successful code parse is not the same as a successful player experience. Both technical checks and human playtesting remain necessary.

## 7.3 Subject-matter review

External AI review can identify issues and create contrasting creative proposals, but AI agreement alone is not independent factual verification. Historical and technical claims require evidence. Medical, financial, political, legal, and vocational simulations need explicit scopes, disclaimers, and specialist review as appropriate. Fictional systems should be labeled as fictional when they might be mistaken for real-world authority.

## 7.4 Intellectual property and branding

The project has discussed federal trademark protection for **DoorQuests** in Class 41 and preservation of appropriate evidence of public use. This paper is not a legal opinion and does not assert a final registration outcome. Future collection names, artwork, sound, narrative premises, and third-party inspirations require ordinary originality and rights review before commercial deployment.

# 8. Visitor Journey and Experience Discovery

A scalable experience universe must help visitors find what suits them without overwhelming them.

## 8.1 Entry layer

The DoorQuests landing page establishes identity, displays featured experiences, and offers entry to the founding collections. The enhanced homepage is an experimental alternative presentation, not a separate governing catalog. Features should remain synchronized with actual published inventory.

## 8.2 Guidance layer

Wortal's Find My Door should respect visitor intent—story, mystery, challenge, game, or discovery—and recommend real, playable pages. A visitor who asks for a game should receive a Gamortal, not merely a generic puzzle. Visitor mood and interaction style can refine the recommendation while preserving an explicit route to browse freely.

## 8.3 Collection layer

Collections should have coherent visual doors, concise genre labels, difficulty indicators where meaningful, and accurate release counts. Lists should never display concepts as if they are live experiences. As the catalog grows, filtering, search, and curated pathways may become more valuable than ever-larger visual walls.

## 8.4 Experience layer

Each experience should communicate what to do, provide an accessible way to begin, retain understandable progress where appropriate, and offer an exit back to DoorQuests. Opening in a new tab is an established site pattern and should be applied consistently when intended.

## 8.5 Return layer

A return visit might be motivated by a daily featured door, a newly published game, a personal best, a different narrative outcome, a topic of interest, or a new seeded dungeon. Repeated visits should be rewarding, not coerced. There is no need to introduce artificial scarcity or compulsory streak mechanics simply to create engagement.

# 9. Development Roadmap and Priorities

## Phase I — Complete the inaugural Gamortals

**Immediate priority.** Build, audit, playtest, approve, and publish Gamortals **#005–#031** individually. Current next-up design work is **The Daily Delve**. Continue preserving the official-versus-prototype distinction and avoid promoting unapproved concepts on the landing page.

**Exit criteria:** Every listed inaugural game is individually functional, founder approved, linked accurately, tested for critical mechanics, and documented. A full catalog count should be verified against actual deployed files.

## Phase II — Evaluate the expanded Discovery Wing

**Future candidate phase.** Review the 12 additional proposed academic disciplines in the context of the existing 15. Confirm what is genuinely distinct, develop representative mechanics, identify evidence and safety requirements, and only then approve pilot experiences and public navigation changes.

**Exit criteria:** A coherent subject taxonomy, verified names, clear learning goals, tested pilot experiences, and a release schedule based on capacity rather than numerical symmetry.

## Phase III — Explore The Guilds

**Longer-term conceptual phase.** Validate interest in applied-technical simulations, compare trade disciplines, consult appropriate subject specialists, and develop a constrained pilot emphasizing the inspect–diagnose–repair–verify loop. Electrical, automotive, HVAC, robotics, and network diagnostics are plausible initial candidates, not a fixed adopted sequence.

**Exit criteria:** A safe and accurate pilot that demonstrates the value of vocational interactivity, a defensible category name, and operational capacity to maintain technical content.

## Cross-cutting work

Throughout every phase: maintain site stability, code backups, privacy/legal pages, accessibility checks, discoverability, quality reviews, usage instrumentation, and honest publication records. New wings should not undermine reliability of the original four collections.

# 10. Sustainability, Operations, and Measurement

## 10.1 Production capacity

The project's rapid initial output makes repeatable development possible, but volume also increases review, correction, and maintenance obligations. A healthy operating model measures **finished, verified experiences**, not simply generated HTML files. Maintenance time should be reserved for browser changes, bug reports, broken links, and educational corrections.

## 10.2 Useful operational metrics

Potential measures include:

| Dimension | Useful indicator | Guardrail |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Reliability | Valid load, functional controls, absence of fatal errors | Don't confuse page views with successful play |
| Engagement | Starts, meaningful interaction, completions, returns | Don't optimize for coercive session length |
| Discovery | Recommendation click-through by intent | Don't promote unpublished experiences |
| Quality | Founder audit results and post-release defect rate | Don't treat AI review as independent proof |
| Accessibility | Keyboard, touch, contrast, audio-alternative passes | Don't claim universal accessibility without testing |
| Content integrity | Verified educational claims and corrected inaccuracies | Don't fabricate confidence |
| Production | Concepts → audited prototypes → approved releases | Don't count planning adoption as delivery |

Existing Cloudflare analytics implementation can support some site-level observation, but this paper does not assert visitor counts, retention, revenue, conversion, or audience fit; no such data has been verified for this document.

## 10.3 Business-model options, not commitments

DoorQuests could later evaluate sponsorship, member features, education partnerships, institutional licensing, custom simulations, or carefully separated advertising. Any monetization model should protect user trust, privacy, educational accuracy, and the core low-friction browsing experience. **No revenue model is adopted here**, and no financial projections are implied.

## 10.4 Major risks

- **Scope expansion:** many good ideas can outrun testing and maintenance capacity.
- **Taxonomy complexity:** too many collection labels can confuse first-time visitors.
- **Content accuracy:** educational and technical simulations require sustained verification.
- **Technical divergence:** duplicated single-file apps can accumulate inconsistent fixes.
- **Weak discovery:** visitors may not locate an experience that fits their interests.
- **Brand and IP issues:** new names and concepts may overlap third-party rights.
- **Score integrity:** local-only achievements cannot provide authenticated competition.
- **Accessibility gaps:** audio-first, reflex, and complex simulation mechanics need tailored alternatives.
- **Audience assumptions:** rapid creation demonstrates production ability, not proven demand.

The response is prioritization and governance, not abandoning ambition.

# 11. Decision Rights and Change Control

The founder retains approval authority for adopting slates, publishing new experiences, making major navigation changes, introducing new collection identities, and altering governance standards. Routine audits and minor fixes should remain traceable. **One file per commit** supports clarity where practical; related changes may require several verified sequential commits.

Recommended decision record for each experience:

- Collection and numbered identifier
- Canonical title and URL slug
- Experience type and difficulty
- Mechanic / interaction thesis
- Origin of concept and substantive inspirations
- Accuracy and safety considerations
- Source / prototype artifact
- Audit results and unresolved issues
- Founder playtest determination
- Publication commit and verification
- Post-release changes / withdrawal history

A daily or milestone journal can preserve the project's chronology, but the repository must remain authoritative for the live technical state.

# 12. Looking Ahead

DoorQuests has reached an unusual point in its early life. The founding metaphor is broad enough to connect storytelling, investigation, puzzles, arcade games, scientific inquiry, social systems, and technical diagnostics—yet specific enough to remain recognizable to visitors.

The long-term potential is significant precisely because the categories are **different**. Entertainment offers emotional and playful reasons to enter. Discovery offers intellectual reasons to investigate. Applied mastery would offer practical reasons to inspect, diagnose, and verify. These propositions can reinforce one another if every release earns its place and if the site remains intelligible.

The next strategic question is not “How many ortals could exist?” There may be hundreds of legitimate candidates. It is instead: **Which door deserves to open next, and what experience must it deliver to justify the visitor's time?**

For now, the answer is clear: complete the inaugural Gamortals while safeguarding the quality of what is already live. Evaluate Discortal's broader academic architecture afterward. Keep The Guilds as a thoughtfully bounded future frontier.

DoorQuests does not need to promise every possible door today.

**It needs to make the next door worth opening.**
