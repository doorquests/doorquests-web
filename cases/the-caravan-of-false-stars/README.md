# Cluortal No. 23 · The Caravan of False Stars

**AD 1021 · Kucha, Uyghur kingdom of Qocho (Silk Road) · Cartographic deception · Hard · 40–60 minutes**

**Signature mechanic: the Star Chart.** The tab teaches steering by a star compass. Each bright star rises at its own point on the horizon and sets at the matching point opposite:

| Star (period name) | Heading |
|---|---|
| The Iron Stake ahead / behind (Pole star; Turkic *Temür Qazuq*) | North / South |
| Rising / setting of the Falling Eagle (Vega; Arabic *an-nasr al-wāqiʿ*) | North-east / North-west |
| Rising / setting of the Flying Eagle (Altair; Arabic *an-nasr aṭ-ṭāʾir*) | East / West |
| Rising / setting of the Scorpion's Heart (Antares; Arabic *qalb al-ʿaqrab*) | South-east / South-west |

You turn each of the 13 nightly entries into a heading. The course is drawn live on a map from Khotan to Kucha (one step per night's stage; "No march" means they stayed put). The check reports how many nights are right. You plot the chart twice:

1. **As written** (new entries in Uyghur script): a diagonal straight across the heart of the Taklamakan. It halts at two wells nobody has seen (nights 4 and 8) and for a "storm" on night 9.
2. **Under the scraping** (revealed by Looking closer at the chart under a low lamp): Vidya's own Khotanese entries. Seven nights north up the dry Khotan River, past the ruined fort of Mazar Tagh, then north-east and east along the Tarim to Kucha. Both routes end at Kucha in 13 nights.

Then you answer where the caravan really camped on night 9: in the poplar woods on the bank of the Tarim.

**Also uses: water and fodder tallies.**
- The water-master's notched tamarisk stick shows six skins filled on *every* night.
- His reed-cord fodder tally shows reeds *cut* fresh every night.

Both are impossible in the deep sands.

**The solution:**
- **The fraud:** Nanaivandak, the Sogdian caravan master, scraped the guide's chart and rewrote it to sell the governor a "road through the sands" for 500 silver. The governor meant to send it to the Idiqut for a spring attack on Karakhanid Khotan.
- **The murder:** Vidya, the old Khotanese guide, refused to swear to the false road. On night 9, under clear stars by the Tarim, Nanaivandak walked him down to the river and came back alone, wet to the knees. Vidya's bronze Vaiśravaṇa amulet, with a snapped cord and river silt in it, is in the master's saddlebag.
- **What a player can get wrong:** they can prove the fraud but accept the "storm" as an accident.

**The header:** a night sky over dunes with camels. A small map shows the claimed road as a straight red dashed line. As you work, the plotted false road (with its "well?" marks), the true river road in gold, and a star at the night-9 camp are drawn in.

## The people

- **Nanaivandak**: the Sogdian caravan master. Charming in four languages. The murderer and forger.
- **Arslan**: his Uyghur clerk. He heard the pumice at night.
- **Sudatta**, 13: Vidya's grandson, the camel boy. Gentleness works; sternness shuts him down.
- **Qutlugh**: the water-master. Half deaf, honest as a tally stick. He goes to the camel market at 3 p.m., taking his tallies with him.
- **Prajñāśrī**: a Uyghur Buddhist monk who keeps travel notes (night 4: the ruined fort; night 9: reading his sutra by starlight).
- **Abu'l-Hasan of Kashgar**: a Muslim pepper merchant and Karakhanid subject, the obvious "spy" red herring. He was cheated like everyone else.
- **Lady Sūra**: a Khotanese widow and silk merchant. She knew Vidya wrote only in Khotanese.
- **You:** the reckoner of Bilge Tutuq, governor of Kucha.

## Endings (7)

1. **The True Stars**: the best ending.
2. **A Name Without Proof**
3. **The Wrong Traveller**: you blame Arslan, Abu'l-Hasan or Lady Sūra.
4. **The Road Through the Sands**: the chart is bought, and the spring army dies in the sands.
5. **A Storm on the Ninth Night**: fraud proved, but the death accepted as an accident.
6. **A Fifth of the Silver**: you take the bribe.
7. **Into the Sands**: you confront Nanaivandak alone, and he flees.

**Ranks:** Master of the stars / Caravan guide / Governor's reckoner / Camel boy / Lost in the sands.

## Notes on history and astronomy

**Real elements:**
- **Politics in 1021:** Kucha was part of the Uyghur kingdom of Qocho (ruled by the Idiqut). Khotan had been taken by the Karakhanids around 1006, making the two rivals.
- **The route:** the Khotan River route north through the Taklamakan to the Tarim, the ruined Tang/Tibetan fort of Mazar Tagh on that river, and the poplar woods (*Populus euphratica*) along the Tarim.
- **The people's world:**
  - Sogdian merchants among the Uyghurs (Nanaivandak is a name attested in the Sogdian Ancient Letters).
  - Uyghur script, which descends from Sogdian.
  - Khotanese written in Brahmi.
  - Vaiśravaṇa as Khotan's guardian deity.
  - Uyghur Buddhist monks with Sanskrit religious names.
  - Notched wooden tallies.
- **Star names:** Turkic *Temür Qazuq* ("Iron Stake") for the Pole star and *Yeti Qan* ("Seven Khans") for the Big Dipper; Arabic names for Vega (the falling eagle), Altair (the flying eagle) and Antares (the scorpion's heart).
- **Star-compass navigation:** steering by the rising and setting points of stars is real, best known from Arab and Pacific navigators.

**Simplifications:**
- **Compass points:** at the Tarim Basin's latitude Vega rises at roughly 35–40° (north-east) and Altair at roughly 80° (east). Antares rises nearer 120° than 135°, so it is rounded to south-east.
- **The Pole star:** in 1021 Polaris was several degrees from the true pole, so "the Iron Stake" is approximate north.
- **The map:** one step on the map is one night's stage, and the geography is simplified.

**Fictional:** the caravan, Vidya, Nanaivandak, every traveller and Bilge Tutuq.
