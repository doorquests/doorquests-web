# Cluortal No. 09 · The Vanishing Jackpot

**1978 · Las Vegas · Casino heist · Medium · 30–45 minutes**

**Signature mechanic:** the Cage Sheet. You balance the cashier's cage, bank by bank, until the missing money shows itself.

**Also uses:** "eye in the sky" surveillance stills and the slot shop's maintenance log, read against the machine's own meters.

## Premise

It's 4:30 am on Sunday, July 9, 1978, at the Silver Mirage, a fictional Strip casino. The Desert Mirage progressive on machine 417 paid **$94,250** at 4:05 to Mrs. Doris Kell. Meanwhile Walter "Pops" Grady, a 71-year-old regular, is in the coffee shop shouting that he hit it first.

The night manager, nervous about a dispute that size, has called the Gaming Control Board. You are Agent Dana Whitlock. The night's paperwork goes on the shred truck at **7:00**, just after sunrise.

## The locked truth

1. **3:38.** Pops hits three Mirages on 417, fairly.
2. **3:40.** Eddie Marchetti, the slot floorman, calls it a "tilt; malfunction voids all pays." Ray Dunn, the mechanic, opens the door to make the tilt look real and **leaves the jackpot standing on the reels**. Pops is walked to the coffee shop for a free steak and eggs.
3. **3:52.** Doris Kell sits down at the machine. She is the comped guest of the cage supervisor, Carl Hendry, and she never put a coin in 417. Eddie "verifies" the jackpot.
4. **4:05.** Carl pays her in **94 plaques worth $1,000 each, plus $250**, and writes the slip as **"paid cash."**
5. **4:10–4:26.** Doris and two friends cash the 94 plaques straight back in at Carl's window, using the names "M. Hale" and "J. Pruitt."
6. The result is that the casino pays the jackpot **twice**. The plaques came back as cash for the ring. The $94,250 "cash" payout existed only on paper, and its $94,000 is in Carl's envelope, hidden under the night's carbon copies in the **sealed shred cart**.

## The Cage Sheet

Each bank's line items appear once you hold the slip that records them. The banks are:

| Bank | Opening | In | Out | Book | Count (4:50) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | $212,400 | +25,000 deposit | −3,500, −6,200, −94,250 jackpot, −31,000, −33,000, −30,000 | **$39,450** | $39,450 ✓ |
| $100 black chips | 4,000 | +35, +62, +40 | −100 | **4,037** | 4,037 ✓ |
| $1,000 plaques | 300 | +20, +31, +33, +30 | −20 | **394** | **300: 94 missing** |

To balance the three banks you need the supporting records:
- the cage sheet, obtained with Sorensen's paperwork authorization
- the pit's fill and credit slips for chip and plaque movements
- the redemption slips and jackpot slip for the relevant banks
- a fresh cage count, ordered by Sorensen

Each bank becomes available for checking once its own required line-item sources and the opening and closing counts have been collected. Checking a figure costs **3 in-game minutes**, whether the answer is right or wrong.

You enter each book-closing figure yourself. Wrong answers give a hint; correct figures become evidence, including the 94-plaque shortfall.

The insight: no plaques were issued to any table after the baccarat pit closed. So the 94 plaques that were cashed in can only have come from the cage itself, which means the jackpot went out in plaques. The cash drawer balances only because $94,250 is written out as a cash payout that never happened.

## Stills and logs

- **Stills:** Cam 7 at 3:38 (Pops at the handle, three Mirages showing), 3:40 (door held open, reels untouched) and 3:50 (Doris seated, reels unchanged). Cam 2 at 4:05 shows five yellow plaque racks on the counter and no strapped cash.
- **Maintenance log:** "3:40 #417 TILT — reset" and "3:43 games 18,214." You open 417 and read its meters yourself. They still show 18,214, so no game was played after the "tilt."
- **Keno ticket:** stamped 3:37 with D. Kell's name. She was in the keno lounge, not at the slots.

## Pressure

- **5:30.** Doris takes a cab to the airport unless Sorensen has security hold her.
- **6:45.** Carl wheels the shred cart to the dock. The **truck comes at 7:00**.
- **Carl's nerves at 3 or above.** The cart and the truck both come 15 minutes early.
- **Opening the cart.** You can open it in the cage before it moves, under Sorensen's order, or on the dock.
- **Eddie's nerves at 2.** He offers you a comp, which leads to the "Comped" ending if you take it.
- **Confronting Carl alone at the dock** leads to "Out the Back Door."

## Connections (12)

**J — the jackpot (who really won)**
- **J1:** Still at 3:38 (look closer first) + Gloria's account → Pops hit it at 3:38.
- **J2:** Keno ticket + Doris's story → Doris was in the keno lounge, not at 417.
- **J3:** Maintenance log + meters → No game was played after the "tilt."
- **J4:** Still at 3:40 + Pops's story → The tilt was staged.

**V — the cage (how it was paid)**
- **V1:** Plaque gap + jackpot slip → The 94 plaques are the jackpot.
- **V2:** Still at 4:05 (look closer first) + jackpot slip → Paid in plaques, written up as cash.
- **V3:** Cash balances + still at 4:05 → $94,000 in cash left the cage with no one to receive it.
- **V4:** Redemption slips + plaque gap → The same plaques came straight back in.

**P — the people and the cash**
- **P1:** Cash in the cart + cart seal → Carl's money is in the shred cart.
- **P2:** Doris's hotel folio + her story → Doris is Carl's comped guest.
- **P3:** Ray's confession + maintenance log → The tilt was a lie, on Carl's plan. (Ray only confesses after J3 is made.)

**R — the red herring**
- **R1:** Gloria's chip + Pops's tip → The chip was a tip, not a theft.

**Strict proofs:**
- **Winner:** J1, plus J2 or J3.
- **Payout:** V1 and V2.
- **Who:** V3 and J4, plus P2 or P3.
- **Where:** P1.

You give your finding to Chief Agent Morrow in Sorensen's office. Morrow's scripted arrival is 15 in-game minutes after the call is initiated (the call action itself costs 3 minutes); otherwise the case advances to the 7:00 a.m. final scene. The player can present the finding in Sorensen's office once Morrow has arrived, or in the final scene.

## Endings (7)

1. **Paid in Full** (best ending)
2. **House Money:** right, but not proved
3. **The Wrong Mark:** you blame Pops, Doris alone, or Eddie and Ray alone
4. **Shredded:** the truck leaves and the jackpot stands
5. **Out the Back Door:** you confront Carl alone on the dock
6. **Comped:** you take Eddie's comp
7. **Tilt:** you rule that nobody won

**Ranks:** Jackpot / High roller / Regular / Tourist / Sucker.

**Source-author testing report:** the supplied Markdown reports scripted playthroughs reaching all seven endings, including an E1 run around 6:35 a.m. scoring 96. These outcomes were **not independently rerun** during this documentation reconciliation.

**Period note:** the Gaming Control Board's Enforcement Division existed in 1978. Nevada's statutory rule requiring casinos to report disputes of $500 or more came in 1983. That's why, in the story, the night manager calls the Board on his own initiative.
