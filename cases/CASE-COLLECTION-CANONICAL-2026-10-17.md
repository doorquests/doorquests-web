# Founding Determination

**The revised thirty-one-case lineup is adopted as DoorQuests' canonical Case Collection baseline.** The selection replaces the earlier thirty-case proposal as the active numbered catalog. It preserves the first published Case, **#01 — *The Nightingale Sleeper***, and maintains **#02 — *The Saint Who Never Existed*** as the intended next case. The remaining cases are approved as concepts and may be developed in the numbered order or in another deliberate production order, without requiring a daily release cadence.

The collection is unified by **investigation**, not by a fixed genre, interface, plot formula, murderer, or setting. Each experience must offer a coherent, discoverable truth that the visitor establishes through appropriate evidence and a distinctive investigative mechanic.

**Canonical promise:** **Find the truth.**

**The governing distinction:** A Case is not simply a Daily Doortal with suspects. Stories explore the consequences of choices; Cases ask players to determine what actually happened and prove a reasoned conclusion. Challenges, developed separately, test puzzle-solving mechanisms.

The revised proposal retains **26 baseline concepts**, moves **four** former baseline concepts to reserve status, and adds **five** new concepts to diversify genre, geography, and participation. A collection of 31 is an editorial target, **not** an obligation to pair each Case with the same-numbered Daily Doortal or publish Cases on successive calendar days.

# II. Canonical Premises and Investigative Design

The premises below preserve the source proposal's narrative hooks and primary investigation designs. They **do not** establish canonical culprits, hidden truths, evidentiary sufficiency, final endings, or solved case dossiers. Those details will be created and reconciled for each Case during production.

## Case #01 — The Nightingale Sleeper

**1936 · England · Railway noir / murder · Hard · PUBLISHED**

A financier is found dead in a bolted sleeper compartment. The Nightingale reaches Aldermouth in six hours, when the passengers will scatter. **Signature:** interview suspects using Coax, Ask, and Press, then cross-reference evidence. **Supporting systems:** investigation clock, suspicion meter, evidence and string boards, deduction/verdict system, chapter saves, alternate endings. **Fixed-crime class:** murder. The existing published HTML and companion README are the source of truth for this Case's implementation. Its difficulty estimate and total playtime are provisional.

## Case #02 — The Saint Who Never Existed

**1187 · Medieval France · Religious conspiracy · Medium · Next proposed build**

A monastery's revered saint has no birth or death record, and the oldest manuscript has disappeared. As a visiting royal archivist, determine who invented the saint and why the fiction has been maintained for two centuries. **Signature:** compare ink, vellum, letterforms, and scribal habits to date manuscripts. **Supports:** an in-game glossary of period scripts and select monastic interviews. **Crime:** forgery and theft; no murder. **Editorial gate:** anchor the deception in a credible human motive such as grants or revenue; the investigation targets forgery rather than faith.

## Case #03 — The Oxygen Ledger

**2142 · Mars colony · Science-fiction sabotage · Medium**

The habitat loses twelve minutes of oxygen while its monitoring system reports no fault; seven residents can access life support. Establish whether the discrepancy conceals sabotage or an emergency rescue. **Signature:** compare oxygen-pressure curves, valve activity, and access-badge data. **Supports:** override history and short interviews. **Crime:** possible sabotage/concealed rescue; no death required.

## Case #04 — Murder Before Breakfast

**1949 · English village · Cozy murder mystery · Easy**

The baker dies before opening. Six residents received similar breakfast baskets, but only one pastry was poisoned. **Signature:** examine recipe cards, ingredients, pantry supplies, substitutions, and delivery sequence to identify the altered basket. **Supports:** delivery route/timeline. **Crime:** murder, handled in a restrained cozy style. **Accessibility aim:** a welcoming entry point for new investigators.

## Case #05 — The Masquerade of Four Faces

**1594 · Venice · Identity deception · Medium**

A diplomat vanishes at a masked ball. Four guests wore the same costume, yet only three exited through the palace gates. **Signature:** logic grid aligning invitations, masks, gondolas, and servant sightings. **Supports:** palace floor plan. **Crime:** disappearance involving abduction and/or impersonation; exact truth to be locked during production.

## Case #06 — Rain on Sunset

**1947 · Los Angeles · Hard-boiled noir · Hard · New**

A studio script girl vanishes after leaving a Hollywood bungalow party. Her sister hires the investigator with forty dollars, a photograph, and one rain-soaked night. **Signature:** drive across a city map with limited fuel and time, choosing which leads to pursue. **Supports:** matches, phone numbers, hostile witnesses, and a tailing sequence. **Crime:** disappearance with a concealed, darker truth.

## Case #07 — The Knots of the Silent Messenger

**1498 · Andes · Inca administrative mystery · Medium**

A relay runner carrying tribute information disappears, while a second quipu conflicts with the official record. **Signature:** learn the game's documented knot positions and values, then decode and compare the cord records. **Supports:** relay-station map and witnesses. **Crime:** embezzlement, with the messenger's fate initially unknown. **Research gate:** historically informed, respectful presentation of Inca administration and quipu systems.

## Case #08 — The Eighth Bell

**1889 · Victorian London · Impossible crime · Medium**

Witnesses hear eight bells from a tower with only seven working chimes; the caretaker is found dead. **Signature:** reconstruct the bell sequence through sound and timing. **Supports:** witness positions on a street map. **Crime:** murder. **Asset gate:** original audio must be confirmed or created; proposed reuse of sound from *The Bell-Ringer of Hollow Vane* is an idea, not an established available asset. Offer an equivalent accessible non-audio investigation route.

## Case #09 — The Vanishing Jackpot

**1978 · Las Vegas · Casino heist · Medium**

A record jackpot is paid to someone who never used the machine, and nightly records are to be shredded at dawn. **Signature:** reconcile cashier cash and chip books until the missing value can be traced. **Supports:** surveillance stills and maintenance records. **Crime:** fraud/theft.

## Case #10 — The Merchant's Empty Chest

**1706 · Edo, Japan · Commercial theft · Easy**

A sealed chest arrives without its silver. Authentic seals and confident guards conceal a substitution somewhere along the route. **Signature:** map the chain of custody and compare weights, seals, handoffs, and warehouse records. **Supports:** porters' tallies. **Crime:** theft by substitution. **Research gate:** Edo-era trading, transport, and sealing practices.

## Case #11 — The Seventh Crew Member

**2196 · Earth orbit · AI mystery · Hard**

Six astronauts awaken, but station logs describe seven occupants and a seventh suspended-animation pod has been dismantled. **Signature:** question an AI that answers within deliberately constrained records and memory. **Supports:** contradictory human recollections. **Crime:** concealment or removal; the missing crew member's identity and fate must be established in the locked truth.

## Case #12 — The Tracks at Coyote Wells

**1881 · Nevada Territory · Western · Easy · New**

A stagecoach robbery is blamed on the Callahan brothers, and the town demands a hanging by sundown. A deputy must interpret the scene first. **Signature:** compare hoofprints, a discarded horseshoe, boot heels, horses, and suspects. **Supports:** deadline and physical-scene reconstruction. **Crime:** robbery with potential wrongful execution.

## Case #13 — The Pharaoh's Second Shadow

**1323 BCE · Ancient Egypt · Royal intrigue · Medium**

Two records of a pharaoh's final audience disagree, suggesting someone entered the palace under another identity. **Signature:** match seal impressions and decipher game-taught cartouches. **Supports:** palace layout and rosters. **Crime:** assassination plot. **Research gate:** use a fictional pharaoh and historically plausible context; do not imply fictional events are established Egyptian history.

## Case #14 — Death in the Second Act

**1912 · Broadway, New York · Theatrical murder · Medium**

An actor dies while performing a staged death that has worked for twenty-seven previous performances. **Signature:** reconstruct backstage movements from call sheets, prop exchanges, and timing. **Supports:** cast interviews. **Crime:** murder.

## Case #15 — Objection at Accra

**1961 · Accra, Ghana · Courtroom mystery · Hard · New**

A young bookkeeper is accused of stealing a cocoa cooperative's payroll. Her inexperienced defense counsel has a single day in court to expose the real thief. **Signature:** challenge witness statements and present the correct exhibits before the judge's patience is exhausted. **Supports:** pretrial file. **Crime:** theft / wrongful prosecution. **Research gate:** Ghanaian courts and legal procedure in the period; believable, fully developed characters.

## Case #16 — Container 409

**2024 · Port of Rotterdam · Maritime crime · Easy**

A container arrives eleven tons heavier than its manifest, its electronic seal intact. **Signature:** calculate and reconcile weights and manifests through the shipment's port sequence. **Supports:** gate-access data. **Crime:** smuggling.

## Case #17 — The Forgery That Was Real

**1926 · Paris · Art authentication fraud · Medium**

A celebrated painting fails authentication; the alleged copy appears older than the authenticated original. **Signature:** use a period-appropriate laboratory bench to compare pigments, canvas weave, and underpainting/X-ray layers. **Supports:** provenance and sale records. **Crime:** art fraud.

## Case #18 — The Message That Arrived Twice

**1943 · Wartime Britain · Espionage · Hard**

Two identical encrypted messages come from different transmitters eighteen minutes apart. One is genuine; the other fabricated. **Signature:** use a taught cipher pad to decode and recognize an impostor's telltale errors. **Supports:** operator rosters and transmitter characteristics. **Crime:** espionage. No real cryptanalytic expertise should be required before entering the experience.

## Case #19 — The Ghost of Chandra Niwas

**1893 · Rajasthan, India · Rationally explained haunting · Medium · New**

A merchant household is driven toward selling its ancestral haveli by footsteps, unexplained voices, and self-lighting lamps. A visiting surveyor has three nights to investigate. **Signature:** test each haunting through physical experiments, sound routes, concealed passages, and lighting mechanisms. **Supports:** correspondence with prospective buyers. **Crime:** fraud by intimidation. **Editorial gate:** treat the family and household as complex people; avoid exotic framing.

## Case #20 — The Senator's Last Banquet

**64 CE · Ancient Rome · Poisoning mystery · Medium**

A senator dies at a banquet though guests drank the same wine and his taster survived. **Signature:** reconstruct seats, courses, and serving order to isolate the fatal delivery. **Supports:** household relationships and incentives. **Crime:** murder. Historical background may include the Roman fire of that year without making fictional allegations about actual persons.

## Case #21 — Room Twelve Was Never Rented

**1963 · American Southwest · Motel mystery · Easy**

A guest is found dead in a supposedly vacant room. The registration ledger, key board, and housekeeper's memory contradict one another. **Signature:** reconstruct occupancy from guest entries and cleaning rounds. **Supports:** diner receipts. **Crime:** murder and/or concealment, to be resolved in the locked truth.

## Case #22 — Last Lap at Mount Panorama

**1987 · Bathurst, Australia · Motorsport sabotage · Medium · New**

A leading driver crashes on the final lap; the team blames driver error, while the survivor insists the brakes failed. **Signature:** compare lap charts and stopwatch pit splits to identify a concealed event. **Supports:** radio chatter. **Crime:** sabotage. **Editorial gate:** fictional event and team at the real circuit; no invented allegations about real participants.

## Case #23 — The Caravan of False Stars

**1021 · Central Asian Silk Road · Cartographic deception · Hard**

A caravan crosses an allegedly impassable route with one traveler missing, and its star chart has been modified. **Signature:** learn to use the supplied chart, then reconstruct the night-by-night route. **Supports:** water and fodder inventories. **Crime:** eventual determination among accident, murder, or fraud.

## Case #24 — The Missing Blue Note

**1928 · Harlem, New York · Musical theft · Medium**

An unpublished recording disappears before a contract negotiation, while musicians disagree about their final rehearsal. **Signature:** compare playable audio takes, tempo, timings, and score markings to establish presence and access. **Supports:** recording-studio logs. **Crime:** theft. **Research gate:** Black musicians are protagonists of their own professional and creative lives; offer an accessible audio-equivalent route.

## Case #25 — Whiteout Protocol

**1997 · Antarctica · Isolation thriller · Hard**

A scientist disappears during a blizzard, but her voice is transmitted two hours afterward. **Signature:** reconcile storm chronology with radio transcripts while managing exposure and exploration time. **Supports:** manipulated scientific observations. **Crime:** research fraud with unresolved fate at the outset.

## Case #26 — The King's Missing Winter

**1315 · Norway · Royal records mystery · Medium**

Three chronicles omit a winter, contradicted by a tax register. **Signature:** align feasts, lunar phases, dates, and taxes to reconstruct an erased period. **Supports:** seals and witness lists. **Crime:** organized historical cover-up. Every required dating convention must be taught in the experience.

## Case #27 — The Voice on Channel Zero

**1954 · Chicago · Broadcast blackmail · Medium**

An announcer receives a purported confession before it has been spoken, and a producer later dies. Two contradictory tape versions survive. **Signature:** compare reel edits, tape splices, and room tone. **Supports:** recording schedules. **Crime:** blackmail and murder; apparent foreknowledge must yield an evidence-backed explanation.

## Case #28 — The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Entry

**1897 · Scottish coast · Maritime murder · Easy**

A lighthouse keeper is found dead and his last log page torn away. Four people visited that week. **Signature:** compare tide tables against reported travel to identify one impossible departure. **Supports:** supply manifest. **Crime:** murder. This concept was earlier proposed as a potential first Case, then retained as #28 after *The Nightingale Sleeper* became #01.

## Case #29 — The Memory Broker

**2074 · Neo-Tokyo · Cybercrime · Hard**

A witness remembers a crime that recorded surveillance disproves; an edited memory is implicated. **Signature:** audit metadata, edit stamps, broker transactions, and sensory gaps. **Supports:** customer records. **Crime:** tampering intended to frame another person. Futuristic technology is fictional; its rules must be explicitly defined inside the Case.

## Case #30 — The Guest in the Wrong Century

**1900 · Vienna · Inheritance fraud · Medium**

A hotel guest presents apparently prophetic inheritance documents dated decades ahead. **Signature:** identify anachronistic typography, stationery, postal marks, and language. **Supports:** hotel and notarial records. **Crime:** fraud; no actual time travel. The forged documents' production method must be credible in the final truth.

## Case #31 — The Island That Sent No Signal

**2038 · Remote North Atlantic · Scientific disappearance · Hard · Closing collection entry**

An observatory continues sending automated all-clear messages for nine days after its research staff vanish. **Signature:** correlate environmental, electrical, access, and transmitter sensor streams to locate when the system's reporting became deceptive. **Supports:** notes from a concealed experiment. **Crime:** cover-up and disappearances. **Collection standing:** a strong final entry, **not** evidence of an adopted interconnected story arc among all Cases.

# III. Editorial Balance and Intended Experience Variety

The lineup deliberately alternates eras, investigative activities, crime types, tones, settings, and difficulty. Not every Case is a murder. A player's duties may involve a royal archive, an investigation aboard a train, a legal defense, scientific logs, sports data, or physical experiments. **A single universal visual skin is expressly rejected.**

| Classification | Canonical planning count |
|---|---:|
| Easy | 6 |
| Medium | 16 |
| Hard | 9 |
| Total | **31** |

**Core diversification intent:** avoid repeated impostor plots, sealed-environment disappearances, and interchangeable logbook/timestamp mysteries. The original source's coverage counts are planning diagnostics rather than immutable quotas. No era, region, or mechanics category is reserved for only one title, and some Cases legitimately use several mechanics.

**Playtime caveat:** The 15–20 / 25–35 / 40–60-minute targets need validation by real testers and may change independently for any Case. Estimated times must never masquerade as observed metrics.

**Case numbering caveat:** Thirty-one Cases and thirty-one Doortals need not pair by number, date, lore, or release schedule. The two collections are separate visitor experiences beneath DoorQuests.

# V. Reusable Case Architecture — Adopted Direction, Not a Mandated Refactor

*The Nightingale Sleeper* supplies a **working reference implementation**, not an instant standardized engine for all thirty-one Cases. The module architecture is an **incremental development direction**: identify repeated responsibilities during Case #02 and subsequent productions; extract shared code only after it is demonstrably reusable and safe.

**Common responsibilities:** canonical truth, evidence state, discoverable facts, time/resources where relevant, action evaluation, progression, save/restore, accessible navigation, results, alternate endings, and QA diagnostics. These responsibilities need not all appear as identical user-interface components or even as identical code structures.

# VI. Canonical Case Production Standard

Every numbered Case proceeds **individually** through concept → truth dossier → evidence design → interactive production → audit → Founder play review → publication/reconciliation. A completed collection-level approval does **not** confer implementation approval on future cases.

## A. Before building

1. **Lock the factual truth.** Name what actually occurred, how, when, where, why, who knows it, and what each misleading account gets wrong. For non-murder mysteries, define the relevant crime or resolution with the same rigor.
2. **Design an evidence-dependency matrix.** Record every decisive clue; its discovery path and timing; whether it is optional, missable, or recoverable; which inference it supports; and which conclusions require it.
3. **Make the signature mechanic meaningful.** The strongest outcome requires using that Case's defining investigative activity. A guessed solution without proof cannot earn the top verdict.
4. **Set a fair red herring.** At least one plausible false conclusion should be rejectable by available evidence; misleading information must be authorially intentional, not accidental contradictory canon.
5. **Teach necessary knowledge inside the Case.** Any historical script, signal convention, cipher, specialist vocabulary, math system, or speculative technology used for a correct answer needs accessible, sufficient in-game instruction.
6. **Plan outcome breadth to fit scope.** Claude proposes five to seven endings as a default. Adopt **meaningful outcome diversity**, not a mandatory numerical quota; a brief introductory Case may warrant fewer endings. Every ending must be causally defensible.
7. **Research and contextual review.** Historical, cultural, technical, and legal systems should be checked to the extent they matter for gameplay. Avoid treating fictional explanations as historical fact or flattening living cultures into exotic set pieces.

## B. During implementation

8. **Shared framework, unique presentation.** Preserve DoorQuests identity while keeping each Case's own art direction, period treatment, controls, narrative style, and sensory design.
9. **Prefer original creative assets.** Commission, create, or program artwork and audio with appropriate rights. Do not assume an existing Doortal contains a portable sound file merely because it has a related scene.
10. **Accessibility is substantive.** Keyboard and screen-reader navigation, sufficient contrast, focus management, reduced motion, small-screen behavior, and non-audio alternatives for crucial sonic clues should be planned into the mechanic.
11. **Keep resource and chronology logic consistent.** Timers, movement, evidence loss, suspects, events, and saved-state recovery must follow the case's actual timeline. An ending may not describe evidence or actions the player did not establish.
12. **Maintain explicit save behavior.** Stable or version-aware local-storage structures, restart, chapter recovery if provided, and retention of discovered endings must be tested when used.

## C. Release and review

13. **Audit endings and key branches.** Run scripted game-state and real browser walkthroughs for the best ending, wrong accusation, insufficient-proof correct accusation, alternate endings, deadlines, and irreversible losses. Clearly distinguish sampled tests from exhaustive certification.
14. **Evaluate difficulty and playtime empirically.** The assigned grade and time window are provisional until practical testing supports them.
15. **Keep HTML and companion documentation aligned.** Companion Markdown must describe the actually published file, true evidence requirements, verified tests, risks, and any unresolved Founder review.
16. **Preserve the publishing discipline.** Publish an `index.html` and companion `README.md` separately, confirming one changed file and commit hash each time; perform homepage/collection link changes in another isolated commit.
17. **Status is evidence-based.** A concept may be **APPROVED** while its truth, playable code, exhaustive QA, and Founder play review remain **NOT YET COMPLETED**.

These rules are adopted now as the **default review framework**. Individual design exceptions and necessary refinements may be reconciled **as Claude produces each Case**, without rewriting all thirty-one dossiers in advance.

# VII. Release Sequencing and Collection Presentation

The numbered sequence follows Claude's proposed mixture of shorter and more demanding Cases, old and future settings, and differing methods of investigation. **No daily Case release cadence is adopted.** A public Cases catalog may initially have one playable entry and expand as each production achieves its own publication baseline.

**Site implementation currently established:**

- **Case #01 — *The Nightingale Sleeper*** is committed as `cases/the-nightingale-sleeper/index.html` with a companion `README.md` in the public DoorQuests repository.
- The DoorQuests homepage contains a featured link and newly open Cases collection.
- The **31 Daily Doortals** remain a separate, fully browsable Stories catalog; an extra experimental interactive story remains distinct.
- Cases #02–31 are **concepts only**. No invented GitHub commits, playable addresses, or completion metrics are assigned to them here.

**Public catalog principles:** Show each Case's title, genre, setting/era, clear availability, and eventually validated difficulty/playtime; avoid implying that unreleased cases can be played. Permit each Case to stand independently, even if future designs include optional callbacks.
