## Premise

Master Renaud of Senlis, a clerk of King Philip's chancery, comes to the Abbey of Saint-Mérence at Corvaise to settle a land quarrel. The lords of Montfaucon say the Forest of Brécy and its mill were taken from their family in 962. The monks say King Charles gave both to their saint in 877, and they hold his charter. On the morning the clerk arrives, the abbey's oldest book, the *Book of Mérence*, has been stolen. He has until Vespers on Day 3, the Feast of Saint Mérence, to give the king's finding.

## The locked truth

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Who invented the saint? | **Abbot Rainard, in the 960s.** He wrote the "877" royal charter and the *Book of Mérence* in his own hand, and raised a nameless brother's bones from the east cemetery in 963. |
| What did the saint protect? | **The Forest of Brécy and its mill.** They bring in 41 livres a year, against 6 livres of offerings at the shrine. The new nave is pledged against Brécy timber. |
| Who stole the Book? | **Prior Étienne.** Thibaut warned him three years ago that the Book matched Rainard's hand. He used the abbot's spare key by beeswax light and hid the Book in Rainard's tomb in the crypt. |
| The Adalgar letter | **A forgery made this month.** Brother Lambert wrote it on the prior's orders, and the prior "found" it at Vespers on Day 1. |

## The Lectern (signature mechanic)

Each document placed on the Lectern has six displayed comparison features:

- **Ruling:** drypoint or lead. Revealed by raking candlelight.
- **"And":** & or ⁊.
- **ae:** ę or plain e.
- **Letterforms.**
- **Ink:** shown as soon as the document is on the lectern.
- **The scribe's hand.**

"And", ae, letterforms and the hand are revealed by the reading stone. The player assigns a date band to each available manuscript as *Before 900*, *950 to 1100* or *Our own century*, and lays two pages side by side to compare hands. The clerk's notes teach the patterns needed to solve this particular fictional collection. These are contextual clues, not universal historical dating laws. The complete inventory contains **22 evidence entries** (including testimony and physical clues), of which only some are manuscript pages.

## Clock and pressure

- Days run from Prime to Compline. A candle buys three more hours at night. You start with 2 candles and can beg 1 more from the cellarer. Entering the crypt by day also uses a candle.
- **Day 1 Vespers:** the prior "finds" the Adalgar letter. Searching Lambert's desk before then catches it still drying.
- **Day 2 None:** old Gerbert falls into a deep sleep.
- **Day 2 Vespers:** Mathieu rides home, taking his petition with him if you don't have it.
- **Nights:** the Book is burned on night 2, or on night 1 if the prior's suspicion reaches 3. A scorched scrap can then be found in the calefactory, and it still shows Rainard's hand.
- **Day 3 Prime:** the prior offers a canon's stall in return for a favourable finding.

## Proof standard for the strongest finding

The four correct findings are necessary but **not sufficient** for E1. The corrected game demands the following evidence-dependent connections and datings:

| Proposition | Required proof in the current HTML |
| --- | --- |
| Rainard fabricated the saint and royal charter | C1 + C2 + C3: charter/register hand, Book or surviving scrap/register hand, and charter/exemplar authentication |
| Brécy land and mill were the motive | C6: account roll + Montfaucon petition |
| Prior Étienne concealed the Book and directed the substitute | C7 + C8 + C11: key/wax, crypt lime/tomb, and prior's note/notice |
| The Adalgar letter is a modern forgery | C10 + C12: letter/psalter hand and letter/old vineyard deed dating comparison |
| Signature document dating | Charter marked *950 to 1100*; Adalgar letter marked *Our own century*; additionally, recovered Book marked *950 to 1100*, **or** surviving scorched scrap corroborated by C2 |

Players may hypothesize throughout their investigation, but the formal king's finding (and the option to certify the charter genuine) is enabled at the **Day 3 Vespers feast**. A correct accusation without the prescribed chain receives E2 rather than E1. The ending narrative is tied to the recovered Book or surviving scrap.

## Endings (7)

1. **The Whole Truth:** all four answers right, the mandatory documentary connections established, and the crucial dating completed.
2. **Half the Truth:** the right thief, but the rest wrong or unproved.
3. **The Wrong Brother:** a different version for Thibaut, Lambert, the abbot or Mathieu.
4. **The Saint Stands:** you certify the saint and the charter as genuine.
5. **Locked in the Crypt:** you go down alone at night after alarming the prior.
6. **The Feast Goes On:** you have no finding to give at the feast.
7. **The Quiet Bargain:** you accept the prior's stall at Senlis.
