# Cluortal No. 10 · The Merchant's Empty Chest

**1706 (Hōei 3) · Edo, Japan · Commercial theft · Easy · 20–30 minutes**

**Signature mechanic:** the Route Board. You trace the chest stop by stop, comparing the weight written in each record against the tally of the men who carried it away. Porters were paid by the load, so their tallies are an independent record.

**Also uses:**
- seal impressions compared against the head shop's seal register
- the porters' and lighterman's tallies

## Premise

In the eighth month of 1706, Ōmiya Tahei, a Nihonbashi cotton and money-exchange merchant, asked his Osaka branch for ten kan of silver in a chest he could see with his own eyes. He wanted the actual metal rather than a bill of exchange, because of the talk of new coinage.

The chest arrives up the river with its paper seals unbroken. Inside is nothing but straw. The two escorts swear it never left their sight.

You are Sahei, a Nihonbashi townsman who runs errands for the constables and is known for his abacus. The Osaka-bound ship *Sumiyoshi Maru* sails at the hour of the Rooster.

## The locked truth

1. **Before the 3rd day.** The Osaka clerk **Seikichi** keeps the branch seal. He seals an empty twin chest with the genuine seal while its border is still unbroken. The twin is plain cedar with four nails to each corner, and weighs 3 kan 300 when empty.
2. **The 3rd.** The branch seal is dropped and a corner of its border chips. The manager writes about it in his letter, and the head shop's seal register shows the chip.
3. **The 6th.** Seikichi packs the real silver: 20 packets of 500 monme, each under the money-changer Kawachiya's seal. They go into a zelkova chest with six nails to each corner, which weighs 3 kan 200 empty and 13 kan 200 full.
4. **The 10th.** The twin chest arrives in Edo on an earlier ship, labelled "samples," held at Izumiya's warehouse "in care of Gonroku."
5. **The night of the 15th.** The real chest spends the night in Izumiya's storehouse. The escorts sleep across the street door. **Gonroku**, the warehouse clerk, holds keys to that door and to the water gate onto the canal. He swaps the chests through the water gate, packs the silver into three cotton bales bound for Osaka, and the next morning writes "out: 13 kan 200" in his book.
6. **The 16th.** Tora's porter carries the chest out **alone, on his back, at the light rate**. The lighterman charges the light rate too.

## The Route Board

| Stop | Weight on record | Carried out by |
|---|---|---|
| Ōmiya, Osaka | 13 kan 200 | 4 men · heavy |
| Settsuya's warehouse | 13 kan 200 | 4 men · heavy |
| *Tenjin Maru* | 13 kan 200 | 2 men on a pole · heavy |
| **Izumiya's warehouse** | in 13 kan 200 · out "13 kan 200" | **1 man · light** |
| The lighter | (not weighed) | 1 man · light |
| Ōmiya's storehouse | 3 kan 300 (empty) | — |

The board asks two questions:
- **Where did the chest go light?** You can answer once every record and tally is in hand.
- **How much heavier is the empty chest than the one Osaka packed?** The answer is 3 kan 300 − 3 kan 200 = **100 monme**, so it is a different chest.

The board answers unlock two connections independently: **W1** requires marking Izumiya as the leg where the load became light; **S2** requires calculating the **100-monme** empty-chest difference. Each correct board answer costs 5 in-game minutes, as does a wrong attempt.

**Strict proofs:**
- **Where:** W1 and W2
- **Seals:** S1, plus S2 or S3
- **Who:** G1 and G2
- **Silver:** F1

You give your finding at Ōmiya before the master and constable Ikeda once the master has summoned him. The summoning conversation costs 8 in-game minutes; his 30-minute travel clock begins when that conversation starts. Otherwise, the final finding scene is presented at the ordinary hour of the Rooster (approximately 5:00 p.m.).

## Pressure (gentle)

- **Hour of the Ram.** Izumiya's Osaka bales go out to the *Sumiyoshi Maru*. You can still have them brought back from the wharf, but this recovery takes **25 in-game minutes** and cannot be started unless there is enough time to complete it **before the scheduled or accelerated sailing**.
- **Gonroku's unease reaches 3.** He slips away, and the ship sails an hour early (about **4:00 p.m.**, rather than 5:00 p.m.). The game records an early sailing if the silver has not been recovered, and the final scene acknowledges it.
- **The master asks you to hush it up** for the sake of the shop's name. Agreeing leads to the "For the Shop's Name" ending.
- **Confronting Gonroku alone at the water gate** leads to the "Into the Canal" ending.

## Endings (7)

1. **Every Monme** (best)
2. **A Light Proof** (right, but unproved)

4. **The Evening Tide** (the ship sails)
5. **For the Shop's Name** (you hush it up)

## Period notes

**Historical background:**
- Edo ran on gold and Osaka on silver.
- Silver circulated by weight (1 kan = 1,000 monme, about 3.75 kg). Money-changers weighed it and wrapped it in sealed paper packets that passed on the strength of the seal.
- Most money moved between the two cities by bills of exchange, so the master's demand for actual silver is unusual.
- Cargo ships ran between Osaka and Edo, and goods were unloaded at Shinkawa.
- Merchant houses kept seals under a responsible clerk and kept registers of seal impressions.
- Edo's time-bells struck the temporal hours: nine strokes at noon, then eight, seven and six.

**Fictional:** Ōmiya, Izumiya, Settsuya, Kawachiya, the ships and all the people. The game gives clock times as rough modern equivalents of the temporal hours.
