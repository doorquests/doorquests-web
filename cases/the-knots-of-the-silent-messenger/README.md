# Cluortal No. 07 · The Knots of the Silent Messenger

**1498 · Hatun Xauxa, Mantaro valley (Chinchaysuyu) · Inca administrative mystery · Medium · 35–50 minutes**
**Signature mechanic:** the Knot Table. An old storehouse keeper teaches you to read khipu cords. Then you read three khipus and compare them.

## Premise

It is midmorning in 1498, early in the reign of Wayna Qhapaq. You are Waman, a runner serving a royal inspector, a *tukuy rikuq* ("one who sees all"). You have carried khipus for years but were never taught to read them.

Asto, a chaski from the first relay post south of Hatun Xauxa, took the valley's half-year summary khipu at dusk last night and never reached the second post. The inspector lodges at the southern tambo at sunset. A llama caravan leaves the high grassland at first light.

## The locked truth

- **What happened.** Two nights ago, Paucar, overseer of a thousand households, had his men carry **100 fine qompi cloths and 100 loads of ch'arki** out of the storehouses by torchlight. Twenty llamas took them to a rock shelter above Rumi's estancia, and the caravan is due to take them north at dawn.
- **The summary had already gone.** Three days ago Yauri, the town's khipukamayuq, tied the summary for Cusco from the true count. Asto carried it south last night.
- **The retie.** After the summary left, Paucar made Yauri untie the official khipu and retie it to match the emptied storehouses (fine cloth 236 → 136; ch'arki 390 → 290). Yauri also retied the food top cord. He could not retie the cloth top cord in time, so it still reads **1,656**, while its two pendant cords now add up to 1,556.
- **The ambush.** Paucar's men waited at the bridge to take the summary back. Asto refused, as a runner should, and fled up the ravine. He hid the cords under the stones of the apacheta at the pass, then fell on the ice. He is sheltering, injured, in a cave by a spring.
- **Red herring.** Kunturi, Asto's partner at the post, has torn hands. They come from hauling the new bridge cables all day.

## The three khipus

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Plain cloth, awasqa (cream) | 1,420 | 1,420 | 1,420 |
| 2 Fine cloth, qompi (red) | **136** | 236 | 236 |
| 3 Maize, loads (yellow) | 2,305 | 2,305 | 2,305 |
| 4 Ch'uñu, loads (grey-brown) | 801 | 801 | 801 |
| 5 Ch'arki, loads (dark brown) | **290** | 390 | 390 |
| 6 Sandals, pairs (blue) | 571 | 571 | 571 |
| Top cord over 1–2 | **1,656** (doesn't add up) | 1,656 | none |
| Top cord over 3–5 | 3,396 (retied to match) | 3,496 | none |

Wayllu teaches three lessons before you can read. Each one ends with a practice cord:
1. Where a knot sits: positions, plus single knots for tens and above (practice cord: 230).
2. Long knots of 2–9 turns, and the figure-eight for a one in the units place (practice cord: 147).
3. An empty space means zero, and top cords hold a sum (practice cord: 1,205).

Every reading you check costs 10 minutes. After two misses, Wayllu hints at the first digits. Correct readings become evidence automatically, and the reading table marks disagreements in red.

## The road

- **Map:** Hatun Xauxa, the storehouse hill, the aqllawasi, two relay posts (chaskiwasi), the river bridge and the southern tambo all sit on the royal road. The pass cairn, the spring cave, the estancia and the rock shelter are on high trails.
- **Walking speed:** about 12 minutes per kilometre on the road, about 20 on the high trails.
- **At night:** after sunset, walking to an exposed high-trail destination (the pass cairn, spring cave or rock shelter) without Rumi's guidance leads to the "Lost in the Mist" ending. Rumi's estancia itself is exempt from this danger rule.
- **Asto's fate is open.** Find him before sunset and he runs again. Find him before midnight and he lives but loses two toes. After midnight he is gone, and the mountain keeps its own count.
- **Pressing Paucar:** at unease 3, his caravan leaves at midnight instead of dawn, and the shelter is empty if you get there late.

## Endings (7)

1. **The Cords Agree:** the best ending. Asto's fate varies with when you found him.
2. **Knots Without Proof:** the account is right but unproven, or given too late.
3. **The Wrong Hand:** you name Wayllu, Yauri alone, or Kunturi.
4. **The Caravan at First Light:** you stay silent at dawn.
5. **Lost in the Mist:** you take a high trail at night with no guide.
6. **A Gift of Fine Cloth:** you accept Paucar's qompi tunic.
7. **Blame the Runner:** you accuse Asto.

**Ranks:** Keeper of the cords / Khipukamayuq / Trusted runner / Apprentice / Loose thread.

## Accuracy notes

**Drawn from the historical record:**
- **Khipu numbers.** These follow the decimal convention Leland Locke identified: single knots for tens and higher, long knots of 2–9 turns or a figure-eight knot for units, and an unknotted space for zero.
- **Top cords.** Top cords that sum their pendant cords are real but uncommon: about 3% of the khipus in the Khipu Field Guide database, and often off by a digit. In the game, Wayllu calls them "not every keeper's habit".
- **Colour.** Colour carried meaning, but there is no known empire-wide colour key. The game's colours are presented as this valley's convention.
- **What khipus could hold.** Some khipus were not numerical. Wayllu says so, and the game doesn't pretend to decode them.
- **Chaski.** Chaskiwasi were spaced a few kilometres apart. Runners announced themselves with a shell trumpet (pututu). There is no evidence that chaski could read khipus; reading was the khipukamayuq's craft.
- **Hatun Xauxa.** It was an Inca provincial administrative centre on the Qhapaq Ñan, in the Wanka/Xauxa homeland. Its hillside storehouses were famously full; the Mantaro valley has over 2,500 qullqa. The storehouses held ch'uñu, maize, ch'arki and cloth.
- **Labor, not money.** The state economy ran on labor obligations (mit'a), not money. Cloth was the most valued gift and a form of payment. Qompi was the fine cloth, woven by specialists, including the aqllakuna. Awasqa was the plain cloth.
- **Rope bridges.** Grass-cable bridges were renewed by community labor.
- **Apachetas.** These are cairns at passes where travellers add a stone.
- **Wayna Qhapaq.** He reigned from about 1493 to 1527.

**Fictional:** all the characters, the theft, the specific numbers, and the detail that this inspector lodges at a particular tambo. The officials' titles and roles are used in their general sense.

**Tone:** the theft is the crime of one official abusing his office. The kuraka (local lord), the weavers, the keepers and the runners are shown as people doing their work faithfully, and the punishments are left to the inspector, offstage.
