# Cluortal No. 14 · Death in the Second Act

**1912 · Broadway · Theatrical murder · Medium · 30–45 minutes**

**Signature mechanics:**
- **The Prop Table.** Two identical daggers can each be hefted, have their points pressed, have their maker's stamps read and have their edges examined. You compare what you find with the property master's props plot.
- **The Backstage Timeline.** Each record and witness places a member of the company on a 9:00–9:30 timeline. The 9:00–9:20 period between the preset check and Cora's pickup is shaded on it; after Moss leaves for the trap room at 9:05, Walter has the documented private backstage opportunity from 9:08 to 9:18.

**Also uses:** interviews with the cast and staff. Each person has a preferred tone (Charming, Plain or Tough) and reacts to evidence you show them.

**Header:** a gilt proscenium arch with velvet curtains and a row of marquee bulbs. On the stage itself, the backstage timeline fills in as the case goes on, with the swap window shaded and the 9:24 stabbing marked. The curtains close as the night runs out. Under the arch, four playbill cards (The daggers, The window, The alibi, The motive) and a countdown to Walter Pryce's cab.

## Premise

The Halcyon Theatre on West 44th Street, Saturday 26 October 1912, at 9:50 pm. In Act II of the melodrama "The Velvet Verdict," Cora Vane stabs Julian Ashcroft with a trick dagger whose blade slides into the handle. She had done it safely for twenty-seven nights. On the twenty-eighth, the blade went in.

You are the sergeant from the West 47th Street station. The company manager is booked on the late train to Chicago.

## The locked truth

- **The daggers:** Rogers & Bates, theatrical cutlers, made two identical daggers.
  - No. 1 is the trick dagger, with a spring blade.
  - No. 2 is steel, dull, and hangs in a sheath on the mantel during Act I. It is never drawn.
- **The killer, Walter Pryce, the company manager:**
  - At about 4 pm he ground both edges of No. 2 on the scene-shop grindstone, saying it was for a letter opener.
  - At 9:08 he went through the pass door with his own key. He came back at 9:18.
  - In between, he put No. 2 on the Act II prop table and hid No. 1 in the Act I sheath.
- **The window:** Moss Kearney, the property master, tested the trick dagger at 9:00, then joined Gus in the trap room at 9:05. Cora picked up the dagger at 9:20 and struck at 9:24.
- **The motive:** Julian had counted the house from the wings and found Pryce's box-office statements 200 to 260 tickets short each week. He meant to tell the producer, Marchbanks, that night.
- **The understudy:** Felix Dorn says he was on stage until the stabbing. The call sheet shows the party guests exit at **9:12**. In fact, the stage-door book shows him out in the alley from 9:13 to 9:27, meeting a man from a rival management.

## The Prop Table

| Test | No. 2 (from the stage) | No. 1 (in the Act I sheath) |
|---|---|---|
| Heft | heavy, solid | light, hollow handle |
| Press the point | doesn't give | slides into the handle on a spring |
| Maker's stamp | R & B · No. 2 | R & B · No. 1 |
| Edge | **freshly ground** (sandstone grit in the blade's groove) | dull silvered tin |

The red herrings are the Act III pistol, loaded with blanks, and the "poison" phial, which holds cold tea.

Once you have the props plot and have pressed the point and read the stamp on both daggers, you can answer "What happened?" The correct answer is **swapped**. Each wrong answer explains why it doesn't fit.

## Period notes

**Real details used:**
- Spring-blade trick daggers
- The props plot, preset checks, prompt book, call board, stage-door keeper and book
- The pass door between the auditorium and backstage
- The box-office treasurer
- The period theatre term "deadwood" for unsold tickets; the fictional case uses this label for a bundled set of torn ticket stubs that corroborates actual sales. The story's bundle must not be mistaken for evidence of unsold tickets.
- Box-office skimming through falsified statements
- Saturday salary night
- The Evening World, a New York newspaper of the time
- Pennsylvania Station, opened in 1910. Grand Central Terminal is avoided because it didn't open until 1913.

**Fictional:** the Halcyon Theatre, "The Velvet Verdict," Rogers & Bates, and everyone in the company.
