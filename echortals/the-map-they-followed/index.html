    To the cartographer,

    Enclosed is the survey of an island no one has yet settled. Its coves, rivers and hills are real; only the names are missing. Ships are already being fitted at Ostrey for families who mean to go there, and every one of them will carry a single chart: yours.

    Draw it as truly as you can. Name what needs naming. Show them the way in.

    — The Chartroom at Ostrey

    You will survey the island, ink its first edition, then return five, fifteen and thirty years later to see what people did with your marks — and what they did without them.

    Progress lives only in this browser tab. Nothing is saved to your device or sent anywhere. Refreshing or closing the page starts the island over.

    Scene 6 · The margin speaks

    Before the Final Edition goes to press, the harbour archivist, Hollis Venn, asks you to sign off six statements for its preface. For each, say where it came from.

    “A chart should know the difference between what it caused and what it merely watched.”

    Six statements about the island

    (a) Your map — a consequence of your first-edition marks. (b) Independent — geography, weather or events that would have happened whatever you drew. (c) Community — a correction or decision the settlers made themselves (even if your map gave them reason to).
