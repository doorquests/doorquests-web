      A note on the Library of Alexandria, and what is fiction here

      The Library. The Library of Alexandria was a real institution of the Ptolemaic kingdom, founded in the early third century BC under the first Ptolemies and closely connected with the Mouseion, a community of scholars supported by the kings. Its collections were papyrus scrolls. Scholars there edited and catalogued Greek literature: Callimachus compiled the Pinakes, a catalogue of authors and their works, and librarians such as Zenodotus, Eratosthenes and Aristarchus worked on texts including Homer.

      What is uncertain. The Library's buildings, the way scrolls were stored, the size of the collection (ancient figures range widely and are doubted) and the circumstances and date of its decline and destruction are all uncertain. The hall and the cabinet in these scenes are imaginative, not reconstructions.

      What is fiction. The sliding cabinet, its scroll cases and their labels, the Keeper, the apprentice and the three examinations are invented for this game. Sliding-block puzzles are a modern pastime (such puzzles are known from the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries); they were not invented in ancient Alexandria, and no claim is made that this challenge existed there.

      Sources: Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Library of Alexandria”; the reference articles “Library of Alexandria”, “Mouseion”, “Pinakes” and “Sliding puzzle”. Full addresses are in the game's documentation.
