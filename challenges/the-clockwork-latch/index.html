How the machinery works
  1. All gears have the same size of tooth, so a gear's radius grows with its tooth count.
  2. Two gears mesh only when they are on the same layer and their axles are exactly the right distance apart: half of the two tooth counts added together. Too far and they don't touch; too close and they can't both fit.
  3. Every mesh reverses the direction of turning.
  4. Speed changes by the ratio of teeth: driven speed = driver speed × driver teeth ÷ driven teeth.
  5. A compound axle carries a front gear and a back gear fixed together: they turn at the same speed and in the same direction. Front gears mesh only with front gears, back with back.
  6. If gears in a loop disagree about speed or direction, the train jams and nothing turns.
  7. Simplifications: no friction, slip, backlash or wear. Speeds are exact fractions.
