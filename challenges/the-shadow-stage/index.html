      A note on wayang kulit, and what is invented here

      Wayang kulit. Wayang kulit is the shadow puppet theatre of Java and Bali: flat, finely cut and perforated leather puppets with jointed arms worked by rods, held by the dalang (the puppeteer) between a lamp and a translucent screen. The dalang moves and voices every character and leads the performance, traditionally with a gamelan ensemble, through stories drawn largely from Javanese tellings of the great epics and from local tradition. Performances could last through the night. Wayang is a living art, still performed, taught and renewed today; UNESCO proclaimed wayang puppet theatre a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2003 and inscribed it on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008.

      What is invented. The workshop beside the theatre, the master's three examinations, the twin lamps, the block sculpture, the cube workshop and the master's sayings are DoorQuests inventions, not historical Javanese theatrical practice. Traditional wayang kulit puppets are flat; a three-view reconstruction from cubes is a geometric game built on the idea of shadows, not a part of wayang. The figure here is an original shape that only evokes cut leather; it does not represent any traditional character, and no symbol in the game has a sacred meaning.

      Sources: UNESCO, “Wayang puppet theatre”; The Metropolitan Museum of Art, collection notes on Javanese shadow puppets; Museum Folkwang, “Wayang Kulit, Javanese shadow puppet theatre”; Smithsonian Folklife, “Javanese Wayang Kulit and Contemporary Shadow Puppetry”.
