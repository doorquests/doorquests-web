## 1. The Founder's Vision

The Founder proposed extending each original DoorQuests experience beyond its browser-based form into a distinct entertainment property. The specific initial vision: every Doortal, Case, Challenge and Game could have its own **20–30-second promotional video on a DoorQuests YouTube channel**, its own suitable merchandise, and other creative extensions developed over time with the Founder and AI collaborators.

This is a **future-facing creative opportunity**, not a direction to interrupt the current build schedule or launch 124 commercial offerings now.

**Core proposition:** Every experience is potentially a seed for a larger original entertainment property. DoorQuests is the destination for interactive participation; some individual worlds may earn deeper investment based on audience interest.

## 3. Short-Form Cinematic Video Strategy

### 3.1 One trailer per experience — where worthwhile

- Produce a distinctive **20–30-second trailer** capturing an experience's atmosphere, central question and invitation to participate.
- Host the videos on a future **DoorQuests YouTube channel**, subject to channel setup, availability and platform rules.
- Consider playlists for **Doortals**, **Cases**, **Challenges** and **Games**, rather than presenting all genres as one undifferentiated feed.
- End with clear DoorQuests branding and an experience-specific invitation to play at `doorquests.com`.
- Respect mystery integrity: **do not reveal solutions, culprit identities, final twists or hidden endings** in promotional material.
- Use original or appropriately licensed images, narration, music and sound effects; follow applicable platform disclosures, rights and content requirements.
- Potentially display trailers on each experience's DoorQuests landing or detail page after performance and privacy implications are reviewed.

**Illustrative example:** *The Nightingale Sleeper* — a midnight train, a bolted compartment, a fractured watch and the hook **“Four suspects. Six hours. One killer.”** The trailer should suggest urgency without exposing the answer.

**Illustrative upper bound:** 124 trailers × 20–30 seconds yields approximately **41–62 minutes** of finished short-form material. This is a portfolio possibility, not a publishing quota.

### 3.2 Future supporting video formats

Genre or seasonal trailer compilations, creator commentary, behind-the-scenes production notes, soundtrack previews, character introductions, puzzle teasers and expansion announcements may be tested selectively.

## 4. Experience-Specific Merchandise

Potential merchandise should emerge naturally from a story world or investigation, rather than simply placing 124 titles on identical products.

Examples include:

- Genre-specific **posters, prints and collectible artwork**.
- **Shirts, mugs, enamel pins and notebooks** with world-appropriate designs.
- **Case dossiers**, replica fictional records, evidence envelopes and printable investigation packs.
- **Puzzles, clue cards, maps and tabletop mystery kits**, if a concept proves suitable for physical play.
- Other collectibles developed in response to actual interest and practical fulfillment costs.

**Commerce architecture principle:** One coherent DoorQuests storefront with experience-specific collections is preferable to opening a separate online store for every property. Print-on-demand and small-scale tests could reduce inventory exposure, but profitability, vendors, taxes, rights, quality and customer service must be evaluated before launch.

Not every experience needs merchandise. Popularity, artistic fit and production economics should guide selection.

## 5. Publishing, Media and Experience Extensions

Individual properties could later support:

- Downloadable detective notebooks, printable evidence, bonus puzzles and collectible case files.
- Short fiction, illustrated novellas, comics, graphic narratives or audiobooks.
- Original music, atmospheric soundscapes and curated soundtrack releases.
- Expanded interactive editions, follow-up chapters, sequels and special events.
- Physical tabletop investigations, puzzles or family games.
- Animated shorts or longer-form screen adaptations where a property gains sufficient traction.
- Selective crossovers, with care not to imply a shared narrative canon where none has been established.

This is an **idea reservoir**. None of these formats is automatically authorized, funded, scheduled or committed for every title.

## 6. Intellectual Property Incubation Model

An individual browser experience can serve as a low-cost initial demonstration of an original entertainment property.

**Proposed sequence:**

1. **Create:** Build and reconcile the underlying interactive experience first.
2. **Publish and test:** Validate core mechanics, narrative coherence, accessibility and actual audience usability.
3. **Promote selectively:** Trial a short-form trailer and measure whether it directs interest back to the experience.
4. **Listen and learn:** Evaluate audience engagement and qualitative feedback, while respecting privacy disclosures.
5. **Expand selectively:** Develop merchandise, additional content or new media only where the creative merit and likely economics justify it.
6. **Repeat and improve:** Preserve the operating principle: **BUILD ONE. USE IT. IMPROVE IT. BUILD ANOTHER. REPEAT.**

**Important guardrail:** Views, merchandise ideas and ambitious portfolio counts must not substitute for a playable, coherent experience. Protect the quality of the Stories and Cases already underway before scaling promotional activity.

## 7. Platform and Brand Boundaries

- **DoorQuests** remains the visitor-facing home for interactive Stories/Doortals, Cases, Challenges and Games and any later experience-specific marketing.
- **The Axiom Continuum** remains credited as creative origin. The proposed **TAC 3.0** broader stewardship/governance architecture is reserved for a later project; this document does not adopt or predetermine that architecture.
- Each experience can retain its own art direction, tone, characters and mechanics while sharing DoorQuests branding and navigational standards.
- Satoshium and BitPangea remain separate projects; their institutional or technical functions are not automatically incorporated into DoorQuests commerce or publishing.
- No assumption is made here about corporate formation, trademark registration, merchandising rights clearance or future revenue.

## 8. Practical Questions for a Future Decision Gate

Before making the strategy operational, evaluate:

- Whether the DoorQuests YouTube identity and handle are available and how channel ownership and recovery are managed.
- A minimum viable trailer workflow (script, artwork, audio, video production, disclosure, quality control, link tracking).
- Costs and time per trailer compared with the benefit of shipping more playable experiences.
- Copyright, music, voices, likenesses, brands, merchandise artwork and any AI-production disclosure obligations.
- Privacy impact of embeds, analytics and outbound-platform links.
- Whether visitors want physical or digital extras and whether a single storefront can be operated sustainably.
- Refunds, fulfillment, sales tax, customer support, supplier reliability and regional shipping constraints.
- Which properties show sufficient organic interest to justify extensions.
- Accessibility, captioning and alternative formats for promotional media.

These are **future research and decision items**, not completed reviews.

## 9. Proposed Pilot — When the Time Is Right

A deliberately small pilot would use one proven property, such as **Case #01 — *The Nightingale Sleeper***:

1. Create one 20–30-second spoiler-free trailer.
2. Confirm the original creative/audio rights and any required AI disclosures.
3. Link its video to the playable Case and assess actual response.
4. Optionally test **one** fitting collectible concept, such as an Art Deco railway poster or detective's notebook, without committing to a full store.
5. Review effort, costs and results before producing another.

**This pilot is not scheduled or authorized for immediate work.** Day #003 planning currently remains focused on **Case #02 — *The Saint Who Never Existed*** and continued case production.

## Closing Reflection

DoorQuests began with gateways to original experiences. The Founder's October 17 idea asks a larger question: **what might grow from a doorway once people begin to care about the world on the other side?**

The answer need not be the same for every Doortal, Case, Challenge or Game. One might become a beloved short adventure. Another might support a soundtrack, a detective dossier, a collectible print, a tabletop mystery or a larger series. The creative opportunity is substantial precisely because the platform allows so many different kinds of worlds to start small.

**Preserve the ambition. Defer the expense. Build the playable experience first.**
